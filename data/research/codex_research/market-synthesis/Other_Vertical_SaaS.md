## Answer

"Other Vertical SaaS" is not a cleanly reported market category, so I'd treat it as the long tail of industry-specific B2B software outside the obvious mega-verticals. On that basis, it is a large, growing, still-fragmented acquisition market.

**Market size and growth.** Public estimates put the global vertical software market around **$147B in 2025**, **$164B in 2026**, and **$283B by 2031**, or about **11.5% CAGR**. If "Other Vertical SaaS" is the residual after healthcare, financial services, real estate, government, education, hospitality, and retail, a reasonable proxy is **$70B-$90B+ of 2026 spend**, growing low-to-mid teens. Cloud/SaaS penetration, embedded payments, AI workflow automation, and industry-cloud replacement of old on-prem systems are the main growth drivers.

**Fragmentation vs. consolidation.** The segment is structurally fragmented because every niche has its own workflow, vocabulary, compliance, integrations, and buyer network. At the same time, consolidation is very active: SEG reported **2,784 SaaS M&A transactions on a trailing-twelve-month basis through Q2 2026**, with **vertical software at 54% of SaaS M&A activity** and PE/VC-backed buyers involved in **59%** of transactions. Translation: the buyer market is consolidating, but the target universe remains highly fragmented.

**Most active consolidators and buyers.**

| Buyer type | Active names | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| Permanent / holdco acquirers | Constellation Software, Volaris, Jonas, Harris, Topicus/TSS, Vela, Banyan Software, Valsoft, Visma, Vitec | Mission-critical niche software, recurring revenue, low churn, founder-owned or lightly institutionalized assets |
| Strategic vertical platforms | Roper, Tyler Technologies, EverCommerce, IFS/WorkWave, Cegid, Trimble, Autodesk/Procore ecosystem players, CoStar | Vertical workflow depth, data, payments, compliance, platform adjacency |
| PE and sponsor consolidators | Hg, Vista, Thoma Bravo, EQT, Bain, Silver Lake, Accel-KKR, Francisco Partners, Genstar, TA, PSG, Alpine, Aquiline, Warburg Pincus, Carlyle | Buy-and-build platforms, category leaders, sponsor-to-sponsor exits, AI/product modernization angles |

**Typical valuation multiples.** For smaller founder-owned vertical SaaS companies, expect wide dispersion:

| Company profile | Typical private-market range |
|---|---:|
| Small, slow-growth, profitable niche software | **2x-4x revenue** or **8x-14x EBITDA** |
| Solid SMB vertical SaaS with recurring revenue, retention, 10%-25% growth | **3x-6x revenue** or **12x-20x EBITDA** |
| Category leader with high retention, payments/data attach, strong growth | **6x-10x+ revenue** or **20x+ EBITDA** |
| Strategic / scarce platform asset | Can exceed these ranges materially |

Aventis' SaaS M&A sample shows long-run private SaaS medians of **4.5x EV/revenue** and **23x EV/EBITDA**, but recent medians were lower: **2.9x revenue in 2024**, **3.8x in 2025**, and **3.1x in Q1 2026**. SEG's Q2 2026 report puts median SaaS M&A at **4.0x EV/TTM revenue**. For a real vertical example, Roper's Transact Campus deal was announced at about **14x expected 2025 EBITDA**.

**Underserved niches and whitespace.** The best whitespace is not "another CRM for X," but the system of record for messy, regulated, paper-heavy workflows. Attractive areas include: specialty trade contractors, inspection/permitting/compliance, fire and life safety, environmental services, agriculture and food traceability, franchise operations, lab and testing workflows, funeral/cemetery management, childcare and elder-care administration, industrial maintenance, field-service microverticals, specialty distributors, and local-government back-office modules.

The most interesting product wedges combine workflow plus payments, payroll, compliance filing, scheduling, quoting, inventory, documents, or benchmarking data. AI helps where it turns unstructured documents, voice notes, inspections, claims, permits, manuals, and compliance rules into workflow automation, but buyers are becoming skeptical of thin "AI wrapper" products.

**What makes a durable, profitable founder-owned niche leader.**

A good target has:

- **Workflow ownership:** users run daily operations inside the product, not just reporting.
- **Mission-critical use case:** downtime, errors, or churn create real operational pain.
- **High retention:** sticky data, integrations, compliance history, trained staff, and customer trust.
- **Recurring revenue quality:** subscription, maintenance, usage, payments, or transaction revenue with low services drag.
- **Vertical depth:** product reflects industry jargon, regulations, forms, pricing, and edge cases.
- **Efficient go-to-market:** reputation, referrals, associations, channel partners, and low CAC.
- **Pricing power:** clear ROI, not a discretionary "nice-to-have" tool.
- **Expansion paths:** embedded payments, analytics, AI automation, compliance modules, mobile field tools, marketplace or network effects.
- **Clean operations:** reliable gross margins, low customer concentration, manageable tech debt, and a capable second layer of management.
- **AI defensibility:** proprietary workflow/data context that makes AI an embedded accelerator, not a substitute for the product.

The investor punchline: "Other Vertical SaaS" is a classic compounder market. The best businesses are not necessarily huge standalone TAM stories; they are small monopolies in narrow workflows, with loyal customers, high cash conversion, and many years of incremental product and pricing expansion.
