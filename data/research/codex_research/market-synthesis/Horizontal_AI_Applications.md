## Answer

**Scope:** I interpret "Horizontal AI Applications" as B2B AI apps that cut across industries/functions: copilots, AI search/knowledge, workflow agents, meeting/productivity tools, sales/customer-support automation, document intelligence, and employee-service agents.

**Market size and growth:** Menlo estimates enterprise generative-AI spend at **$37B in 2025**, up **3.2x YoY**. The application layer was **$19B**, with **horizontal AI at $8.4B**, growing **5.3x YoY**. That is current spend, not ultimate TAM. McKinsey's broader value pool is far larger: **$2.6T-$4.4T** in annual economic potential from genAI use cases, with most value in customer operations, sales/marketing, software engineering, and R&D. A practical underwriting view: near-term software spend is in the low tens of billions; long-run addressable opportunity is much larger but will be captured unevenly by incumbents, AI-native startups, and services automation.

**Fragmentation vs consolidation:** The segment is fragmented at the app layer but consolidating at distribution/control points. Menlo says AI application startups captured **63%** of app-layer revenue in 2025, while incumbents remain stronger where reliability, compliance, data governance, and system-of-record integrations matter. Horizontal copilots are already relatively concentrated around Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and enterprise knowledge platforms. The long tail of workflow agents, sales tools, AI calendars, meeting tools, support bots, document tools, and vertical overlays is highly fragmented. Expect consolidation because incumbents need AI roadmaps, domain datasets, and workflow depth, while PE-backed roll-ups can spread AI engineering across acquired vertical-software portfolios.

**Most acquisitive buyers:** Active strategic buyers include **ServiceNow, Salesforce, Workday, Thomson Reuters, Atlassian, Dropbox, Microsoft, Intuit, Adobe, SAP, Oracle, Zoom, and data/security platforms**. In vertical-software roll-ups, the most relevant acquirers include **Constellation Software/Volaris/Jonas/Topicus, Valsoft, Banyan Software, Visma, Roper, EverCommerce**, and PE-backed platforms. Financial sponsors active around this thesis include **Thoma Bravo, Vista, Hg, TA, Insight, Accel-KKR, Francisco Partners, K1, PSG, Warburg Pincus, Silver Lake**, and growth/late-stage funds backing AI-native platforms.

**Valuation multiples:** For disclosed SaaS M&A, SEG reports **median EV/TTM revenue of ~4.0x in 2Q26**, with scarce assets above that. Public SaaS medians reset lower: SEG's public SaaS index fell to **3.2x EV/TTM revenue** in 2Q26, while vertically focused public SaaS was **3.7x**. SEG's 2026 annual report cites **~29.7x EBITDA** for B2B SaaS at year-end 2025, but EBITDA data is sparse. For SMB/founder-owned vertical software, a sensible market range is **~3-6x ARR** for durable lower-middle-market SaaS, **5-8x+ ARR** for strong retention/growth/proprietary data, and **~8-15x EBITDA** for profit-led PE/platform deals. AI alone does not create a premium; buyers now diligence whether AI improves retention, expansion, gross margin, and defensibility.

**Recent notable M&A:**
- **ServiceNow / Moveworks**, 2025: $2.85B for enterprise employee-support AI agents.
- **Workday / Sana**, 2025: ~$1.1B for enterprise knowledge, learning, and AI work experiences.
- **Workday / Evisort**, 2024: AI contract/document intelligence.
- **Thomson Reuters / Casetext**, 2023: $650M for legal AI and CoCounsel.
- **Salesforce / Tenyx**, 2024: conversational AI for customer service/voice.
- **Atlassian / Loom**, 2023: ~$975M for async video collaboration with AI summarization/workflow potential.
- **Atlassian / The Browser Company**, 2025: reported $610M, centered on AI browser/work interface.
- **Dropbox / Reclaim.ai**, 2024: AI scheduling and productivity.

**Underserved niches and whitespace:** The best whitespace is not generic "AI assistant for X"; it is AI embedded into painful, regulated, document-heavy, or labor-heavy workflows with measurable ROI. Attractive areas include SMB back-office automation, compliance and audit workflows, field-service dispatch, construction documentation, government permitting, insurance claims, revenue-cycle/admin healthcare, specialty legal/tax/accounting workflows, vertical customer support, procurement/vendor risk, and industry-specific knowledge management. Menlo notes vertical AI adoption is strongest in historically underserved sectors with manual, unstructured workflows.

**What makes a durable founder-owned niche leader:** Buyers will pay for mission-critical workflow ownership, high gross retention, expansion revenue, proprietary workflow/data exhaust, compliance credibility, low churn, clean financials, and a wedge into a non-obvious customer segment. The durable AI version has AI in the workflow core, not as a feature wrapper; clear human-in-the-loop controls; provable customer ROI; integrations into the system of record; inference costs that do not destroy gross margin; and a founder/operator culture close enough to customers to keep compounding product depth.
