## Answer

**Scope:** I'm interpreting "Security" as vertical-market software for the physical security industry: alarm dealers, commercial security integrators, guard companies, monitoring centers, access control/video/visitor-management operators, public-safety/security operations, and fire/life-safety adjacencies. This is different from horizontal cybersecurity software.

**Market size and growth:** The broad physical-security ecosystem is large: roughly **$120B+ global TAM** growing at a **mid-single-digit CAGR**, while video intelligence alone is cited at **$60B+ TAM** growing around **10% CAGR**. The narrower "pure software" slice is much smaller but faster-growing: guard-management software alone is estimated around **$1.6B in 2024**, growing to **$4.5B by 2033** at about **12% CAGR**. A practical estimate for the vertical-software opportunity across guard workforce management, alarm/central-station software, cloud VMS, access control SaaS, visitor management, incident reporting, and compliance/workflow software is **low-to-mid single-digit billions today**, growing high-single to low-teens.

**Fragmentation vs. consolidation:** This is still **highly fragmented at the operator and channel layer**. Thousands of local alarm dealers, monitoring companies, guard firms, integrators, and fire/security service providers run on a patchwork of vertical apps, QuickBooks, spreadsheets, legacy central-station systems, scheduling tools, and vendor portals. Consolidation is much more advanced in **fire/life-safety, commercial integration, guard services, and platform hardware/software ecosystems** than in SMB vertical workflow software. Capstone reported **242 Security Solutions M&A deals in 2025**, up **24.1% YoY**, with PE add-ons the largest buyer cohort.

**Most active buyers and consolidators:**

| Buyer type | Active names |
|---|---|
| PE-backed security/service roll-ups | **Pye-Barker Fire & Safety**, **Everon / GTCR**, **RapidFire Safety & Security**, **Security Franchisors / Gemspring**, **Allied Universal**, **GardaWorld**, **Convergint** |
| Strategic physical-security platforms | **Motorola Solutions**, **Alarm.com**, **Honeywell / LenelS2**, **Johnson Controls**, **ASSA ABLOY / HID**, **Acre Security**, **Genetec**, **Eagle Eye / Brivo** |
| Vertical software / PE buyers | **K1** via Trackforce/TrackTik, **Thoma Bravo** via Everbridge, **Riverside** via Omnigo, plus broad VMS acquirers such as **Constellation/Harris**, **Volaris**, **Banyan**, **ASG/Alpine**, and **TA/Francisco/Accel-KKR** where assets have enough recurring revenue and niche dominance |

**Typical valuation multiples:** For small founder-owned security vertical software, likely clearing ranges are:

| Asset profile | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Services-heavy security integrator / monitoring / fire-security business | ~**1-2.5x revenue**, **8-12x EBITDA** |
| Niche profitable SMB vertical software with moderate growth | ~**2-4x ARR/revenue**, **10-16x EBITDA** |
| Scaled SaaS platform with strong retention, high recurring revenue, and double-digit growth | ~**4-7x ARR/revenue**, sometimes higher |
| Category leader with AI/video data moat, cloud architecture, strategic buyer tension | **7-10x+ ARR** possible, but selective |

Capstone's five-year security-solutions average was **2.2x EV/revenue** and **11.8x EV/EBITDA**. Broader private software data from Aventis shows software M&A medians around **3.7x EV/revenue** and **19x EV/EBITDA**, with smaller deals generally lower.

| Feb-Mar 2025 | **Motorola acquired RapidDeploy and agreed to acquire InVisit** | Public-safety cloud software plus visitor management for Avigilon Alta |

**Whitespace:** The best niches are where "security" is becoming software-defined but buyers are still stuck with local-service workflows. Attractive gaps include SMB security integrator ERP, guard scheduling/payroll/billing/client proof-of-service, false-alarm reduction, remote video monitoring operations, cyber-physical compliance, access-control identity governance, school/multifamily/healthcare/data-center security workflows, and fire/life-safety inspection compliance. The biggest near-term pull is the convergence of physical security with IT/CISO ownership: buyers increasingly want auditability, identity integrations, risk reporting, and managed services, not just cameras and badge readers.

**What makes a durable founder-owned niche leader:** The best businesses are mission-critical, recurring, and deeply embedded in daily operations. Look for high gross retention, low logo churn, sticky integrations into panels/cameras/access systems/payroll/accounting/CAD/monitoring centers, domain-specific compliance content, and a product that saves labor in a labor-constrained industry. The strongest companies also own a channel wedge: dealers, integrators, monitoring centers, or security firms already trust them, which lowers CAC and makes the product hard to displace.
