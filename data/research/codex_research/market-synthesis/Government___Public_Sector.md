## Answer

### Market Size And Growth

The Government & Public Sector vertical-software market is attractive but definition-sensitive.

A narrow "government software" view is roughly a **$25B-$35B global software market today**, with high-single to low-double-digit growth. SEG cites government software license, maintenance, and subscription revenue at **$28.2B**, while DataIntelo estimates **$26.4B in 2025 growing to $55.8B by 2034**, an **8.7% CAGR**. Broader "GovTech" definitions that include services, hardware, cloud, and smart-government platforms are much larger and faster-growing: Mordor estimates smart government at **$58.3B in 2026 growing to $139.2B by 2031**, about **19% CAGR**.

The segment has unusually durable demand because software touches legally required workflows: budgeting, procurement, permitting, public records, courts, public safety, tax, benefits, licensing, HR, inspections, emergency response, and citizen communications. Budget cycles can slow purchasing, but once implemented, switching tends to be painful.

### Fragmentation Vs. Consolidation

This market is **fragmented at the workflow and customer level, but consolidating at the platform level**.

On the customer side, the U.S. alone has about **90,837 local governments** as of the 2022 Census of Governments, before considering state agencies, federal agencies, courts, school districts, utilities, and special districts. Each buyer has different statutes, procurement rules, forms, integrations, and reporting obligations.

On the vendor side, the market remains full of point solutions because each workflow is specialized. SEG breaks the vertical into categories such as ERP/admin, community services, infrastructure/development management, legal/compliance, public safety dispatch, RMS, surveillance/communications, and workforce management.

But consolidation is real. Large platforms are buying niche products to expand suites, cross-sell into existing agencies, and modernize legacy on-premise systems. Tyler calls itself the broadest public-sector software portfolio, Granicus has reportedly made at least 14 acquisitions since 2016, and PE-backed platforms such as NEOGOV, CivicPlus, Euna, and CentralSquare continue to aggregate category-specific products.

### Most Active Buyers And Roll-Ups

The most important acquirers to watch:

- **Tyler Technologies**: the public pure-play leader in U.S. public-sector software. Active across courts, ERP, public safety, tax, permitting, schools, data, and digital government. Recent acquisitions include ResourceX, ARInspect, MyGov, CloudGavel, For The Record, and CODY Systems.
- **Granicus**: PE-backed by Vista/Harvest; consolidates citizen engagement, digital services, meetings/agendas, records, permitting, and destination/community experience software. Acquired Simpleview in 2024.
- **CivicPlus**: local-government websites, communications, ordinances, records, accessibility, engagement, and resident-experience software. Has acquired Municode, SeeClickFix, Optimere, and Streamline.
- **NEOGOV**: public-sector HR, workforce, compliance, learning, policy, and public-safety workforce software. EQT and CPP Investments agreed to acquire it in 2025 from Warburg Pincus/Carlyle; NEOGOV also bought PowerDetails in 2026.
- **Euna Solutions**: public-sector finance, budgeting, procurement, grants, payments, and compliance software; GI Partners-backed.
- **OpenGov / Cox Enterprises**: OpenGov became a Cox-controlled platform at a reported **$1.8B valuation** in 2024; suite spans ERP, budgeting, procurement, permitting, asset management, and citizen services.
- **Motorola Solutions, Axon, Hexagon, CentralSquare**: especially active around public safety, evidence, dispatch, emergency communications, and records.
- **Civica, Harris/Constellation, Valsoft**: important non-U.S. or long-tail vertical-software consolidators, especially where cash-flowing niche products can be run for decades.

### Valuation Multiples

Current SaaS multiples are lower than 2021, but good GovTech assets still earn premiums when they are mission-critical and sticky.

As of SEG's 2Q26 report, the public SaaS index median was **3.2x EV/TTM revenue**, vertically focused SaaS was **3.7x**, and disclosed private SaaS M&A was around **4.0x median EV/TTM revenue**. SEG also says vertical software represented **54% of SaaS M&A activity** in 2Q26, and government was about **7% of vertical SaaS activity**.

For SMB public-sector vertical software, a practical range is:

- **2x-4x revenue** or **7x-12x EBITDA** for slower-growth, services-heavy, smaller, or on-prem-heavy businesses.
- **4x-7x revenue** or **12x-18x EBITDA** for profitable SaaS with strong retention, clean financials, and repeatable implementation.
- **8x+ revenue / 20x+ EBITDA** only for scarce scaled assets with category leadership, high net retention, strong growth, proprietary data, and real platform value.

Notable large deals show where premiums appear: OpenGov at **$1.8B**, NEOGOV reportedly **>$3B**, and Tyler's For The Record deal at **$258M enterprise value**.

### Recent Notable Transactions

- **Tyler acquired ResourceX and ARInspect** in October 2023, adding budgeting and AI inspection capabilities.
- **Cox Enterprises acquired majority ownership of OpenGov** in February 2024 at a reported **$1.8B valuation**.
- **Granicus acquired Simpleview** in September 2024, moving into destination/community experience software.
- **Tyler acquired MyGov** in January 2025, expanding permitting, inspections, planning, zoning, and code enforcement.
- **EQT and CPP Investments agreed to acquire NEOGOV** in July 2025; reports put value above **$3B**.
- **CivicPlus acquired Streamline** in December 2025, adding digital solutions for 2,500+ public agencies.
- **Tyler agreed to acquire For The Record** in February 2026 for **$258M enterprise value**, strengthening court recording.
- **NEOGOV acquired PowerDetails** in April 2026, adding off-duty law-enforcement scheduling, billing, and compliance.
- **Tyler acquired CODY Systems** in July 2026, adding public-safety data management and information sharing.

### Underserved Niches And Whitespace

The best whitespace is not "generic government AI." It is boring, regulated workflow automation where agencies still use email, PDFs, spreadsheets, and legacy databases.

Promising niches include:

- **Special districts**: water, sewer, fire, transit, housing authorities, parks, libraries, mosquito/vector control, cemetery districts.
- **Grant compliance and reporting**: IIJA, federal grants, subrecipient monitoring, audit readiness, outcome measurement.
- **Long-tail permitting and inspections**: small municipalities need cheaper, faster alternatives to enterprise permitting suites.
- **Public records, FOIA, redaction, retention, and accessibility**: rising volume, statutory deadlines, ADA/Section 508 pressure.
- **Public safety administration**: off-duty scheduling, accreditation, warrants, evidence chain-of-custody, training, overtime, wellness.
- **Procurement and vendor compliance**: cooperative purchasing, local vendor inclusion, bid evaluation, contract lifecycle, spend analytics.
- **Cybersecurity/compliance for small agencies**: CJIS, NIST, identity, backups, incident readiness, vendor-risk automation.
- **Interoperability layers**: connectors across Tyler, Accela, CivicPlus, Granicus, Esri, Microsoft, state portals, payments, and document systems.

### What Makes A Durable Founder-Owned Niche Leader

A strong founder-owned company here usually has:

- A narrow, painful, mandated workflow with statutory deadlines or audit exposure.
- Deep domain content: forms, rules, templates, reporting logic, integrations, and implementation playbooks.
- High logo retention, low churn, annual upfront billing, and referenceable public-sector customers.
- A productized implementation motion, not endless custom services.
- Cooperative contracts, security documentation, accessibility readiness, and procurement-friendly packaging.
- Integrations into the systems agencies already use: GIS, finance, identity, payments, document management, state/federal reporting.
- A capital-efficient model with modest but steady growth, strong gross margins, and **20%-35% EBITDA potential** once support and implementation are disciplined.
- AI used surgically: form extraction, redaction, routing, summarization, anomaly detection, and compliance checks, not a vague chatbot bolted onto a weak workflow.

The durable niche leader is usually not the flashiest vendor. It is the one that becomes part of how a department stays compliant every week.
