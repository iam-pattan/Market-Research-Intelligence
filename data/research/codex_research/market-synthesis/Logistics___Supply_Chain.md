## Answer

**Market size and growth:** Logistics & supply-chain B2B software is a large, growing, but uneven market. A reasonable 2026 TAM frame is **$30B-$40B+ globally** for broad SCM software, with adjacent submarkets growing at different speeds: Mordor estimates **SCM software at $36.4B in 2026, growing 9.0% CAGR to $56.0B by 2031**; Fortune Business Insights estimates **logistics software at $17.5B in 2026, growing 7.75% CAGR to $31.7B by 2034**; WMS is smaller but faster, with Mordor estimating **$4.8B in 2026, growing 18.0% CAGR to $10.9B by 2031**.

**Fragmentation vs. consolidation:** The market is **moderately fragmented at the suite level and highly fragmented in vertical/SMB workflows**. Large suites include SAP, Oracle, Blue Yonder, Manhattan, Kinaxis, Descartes, WiseTech, E2open, Coupa, Körber, Infor, and Epicor, but day-to-day workflows remain split across TMS, WMS, freight audit, customs, supplier risk, routing, broker tools, yard management, cold chain, returns, visibility, EDI, procurement, and industry-specific systems. This creates classic vertical-software roll-up conditions: sticky workflows, dated incumbents, local/regulatory specialization, and many subscale vendors.

**M&A activity is active and strategic.** Lincoln International counted **167 SCM software M&A deals in 2024**, up from 159 in 2023 and 144 in 2022, and noted continued momentum in Q1 2025. The main consolidation logic is platform convergence: buyers want planning + execution + visibility + compliance + AI/data in one stack. Hampleton similarly says SCMS M&A rebounded in 1H 2025 after a 2024 dip, with AI, robotics, cybersecurity, ESG, and buy-and-build themes supporting activity.

**Most active consolidators/buyers:**
- **Strategics / platforms:** Descartes, WiseTech Global, Blue Yonder/Panasonic, Aptean, Manhattan, Oracle, SAP, Infor, Epicor, Körber, Trimble, Fleetio, Samsara, Kinaxis, E2open/WiseTech, Coupa.
- **PE and sponsor-backed buyers:** Vista Equity, Thoma Bravo, Francisco Partners, TA Associates, Hg, EQT, CVC/CD&R, Battery, Rubicon Technology Partners, Banneker Partners, Sumeru, LLR, and vertical software platforms such as Aptean/Epicor-style consolidators.

**Valuation multiples:** For private SaaS broadly, Aventis' 2015-2026 sample shows **median 4.5x EV/revenue and 23.0x EV/EBITDA**, but recent multiples are lower: **2.9x median EV/revenue in 2024, 3.8x in 2025, and 3.1x in Q1 2026**. For **SMB vertical logistics software**, a practical underwriting range is usually:
- **2x-4x revenue** for slower-growth, services-heavy, smaller or mixed SaaS/license businesses.
- **4x-7x+ revenue** for clean SaaS with strong retention, growth, and scarce workflow/data assets.
- **8x-15x EBITDA** for profitable but slower founder-owned assets.
- **15x-25x+ EBITDA** for scarce, scaled, mission-critical platforms.
Marquee deals can go higher: Lincoln cites **Avetta at 12.0x revenue / 30.0x EBITDA**, **JAGGAER at 7.4x revenue / 20.0x EBITDA**, and **Logility at 3.9x revenue / 39.0x EBITDA**.

**Recent notable transactions:**
- **WiseTech / E2open, 2025:** $2.1B acquisition of a global trade and supply-chain platform.
- **Aptean / Logility, 2025:** roughly $490M EV, adding AI-first planning and inventory optimization.
- **Descartes / 3GTMS, 2025:** about $115M for transportation management software.
- **Descartes / Tai Software, 2026:** about $100M for freight broker TMS.
- **Blue Yonder / One Network Enterprises, 2024:** about $839M for multi-enterprise supply-chain network capabilities.
- **EQT / Avetta, 2024:** about $3B for supplier risk/compliance software.
- **Vista / JAGGAER, 2024:** major source-to-pay and supplier collaboration acquisition.
- **Francisco Partners / Command Alkon, 2026:** sponsor-to-sponsor sale of heavy building materials logistics/operations software.

**Underserved niches / whitespace:**
- **SMB and mid-market cloud WMS/TMS**, especially for 3PLs, regional distributors, and freight brokers still using spreadsheets or old on-prem systems.
- **Trade compliance, customs, tariff impact, denied-party screening, UFLPA/forced-labor traceability, digital product passports, and supplier ESG evidence.**
- **Verticalized logistics workflows** in healthcare/pharma cold chain, hazmat, food/ag, building materials, heavy equipment, returns/reverse logistics, project logistics, and cross-border Mexico/Canada trade.
- **Freight audit/payment, dock scheduling, yard management, claims, carrier onboarding, procurement, and spot/RFP optimization.**
- **AI execution layers** that sit across ERP/WMS/TMS/telematics/EDI and resolve exceptions, not just dashboards.
- **Cybersecurity and vendor-risk tooling** for connected supply-chain networks.

**What makes a durable, profitable founder-owned niche leader:** best targets solve an expensive operational problem with clear ROI; own a narrow workflow deeply; have embedded integrations into ERP, WMS, TMS, EDI, telematics, marketplaces, carriers, or regulators; carry proprietary transaction/network data; show low churn and high renewal discipline; can charge implementation/services without becoming a consulting shop; and serve a niche where domain complexity protects against generic SaaS or AI tools. The truly attractive ones feel "boring" from the outside but are mission-critical inside the customer's daily operating rhythm.
