## Answer

As of September 2026, "Dev Tools, Data & AI Infrastructure" is best viewed as a stack, not a single SIC-code market: DevOps/DevSecOps, CI/CD, testing, observability, infrastructure automation, data integration/quality/catalog/governance, MLOps/model ops, vector/RAG infrastructure, and enterprise-ready open-source databases.

**TAM and growth.** The investable software TAM is already very large, but easy to double-count. A useful triangulation:

| Layer | Market signal |
|---|---:|
| DevOps | Research and Markets estimates DevOps at **$18.8B in 2026**, growing to **$47.1B by 2030**, a **25.8% CAGR**. |
| Data & analytics software | Grand View Research estimates data/analytics software at roughly **$205.6B in 2026**, reaching **$345.3B by 2030**, a **13.6% CAGR**. |
| AI infrastructure | The Insight Partners estimates AI infrastructure at **$62.1B in 2025**, reaching **$510.0B by 2034**, a **26.35% CAGR**. This includes hardware/cloud components, so the pure software slice is smaller. |
| SaaS/Data/DevOps M&A proxy | SEG says Analytics & Data Management was one of the most active SaaS M&A categories in 2025, while DevOps & IT Management carried premium public SaaS multiples. |

My practical estimate: **$250B+ current broad software spend** across data/dev/AI infra if analytics software is included, with a cleaner "developer/data/AI infra tooling" serviceable pool probably in the **$75B-$125B** range today and growing mid-teens to high-20s depending on subsegment.

**Fragmentation vs. consolidation.** The segment is **fragmented at the tool level** and **consolidating at the enterprise platform level**. Fragmentation persists because open source, cloud APIs, model churn, and new developer workflows keep creating new wedges. But buyers increasingly want fewer vendors, unified telemetry, security, governance, and AI-ready data context. That is why large platforms are buying aggressively: Cisco/Splunk, IBM/HashiCorp, Databricks/Tabular and Neon, Snowflake/Crunchy Data, Datadog/Metaplane and Adaptive ML, Atlassian/DX, JFrog/Qwak.

**Typical valuation multiples for SMB / founder-owned assets.** Public and large strategic deals can distort expectations, so for founder-owned SMB software I'd underwrite like this:

| Company profile | Typical range |
|---|---:|
| Mature niche tool, low growth, small ARR, customer concentration | **1.5x-3.5x revenue** or **5x-8x EBITDA** |
| Profitable vertical/dev/data SaaS, 10%-25% growth, sticky workflow | **3x-6x ARR/revenue** or **8x-15x EBITDA** |
| Best-in-class infra tool, >25%-30% growth, NRR >115%-120%, clear AI/data/security relevance | **6x-10x+ ARR** or **15x-25x+ EBITDA** |
| Scarce strategic asset with open-source standard, data gravity, or platform-control value | Can exceed normal private-market ranges |

**Underserved niches / whitespace.**

The best whitespace is not "another dashboard." It is tooling that makes AI-era software/data systems reliable, governable, and cheaper to operate:

- **AI agent governance for engineering teams:** permissions, sandboxing, approval trails, secrets handling, repo access control, and auditability.
- **AI-generated code risk management:** provenance, dependency drift, code review triage, vulnerability prioritization, and compliance evidence.
- **Data observability for AI pipelines:** lineage from source data to prompt/RAG output/model action, with evaluation and rollback.
- **Inference FinOps:** cost/performance governance at model, agent, user, token, GPU, workload, and business-process level.
- **Private/hybrid/on-prem AI infrastructure:** regulated enterprises still need tooling that works behind the firewall.
- **Vertical data/dev platforms:** fintech, healthcare, defense, industrial, insurance, pharma, and public sector workflows remain under-tooled.
- **Postgres ecosystem tools:** migrations, compliance, tuning, pooling, backup, edge sync, and AI-app operational data.
- **Test data, synthetic data, and environment management:** especially for regulated teams and legacy systems.
- **API contract testing and runtime security for agentic systems:** agents create new API and permission failure modes.
- **Legacy modernization with AI-safe controls:** COBOL/mainframe, ETL modernization, and regression testing are still deeply painful.

**What makes a durable, profitable, founder-owned niche leader.**

A strong founder-owned asset here usually has: a painfully specific buyer problem, deep workflow embedment, high gross margins, low churn, measurable ROI, clear integrations into CI/CD/data/cloud stacks, and a reputation with practitioners. The best ones often start narrow: "we solve this ugly production problem better than anyone," then expand into adjacent controls, governance, reporting, or automation.

Durability comes from **switching cost plus trust**: production usage, compliance evidence, audit trails, historical telemetry, integration depth, and a product that becomes part of the customer's operating muscle memory. Profitability comes from disciplined scope, self-serve/onboarding where possible, high-quality docs/community, low support burden, and avoiding custom-services gravity.

The acquirer story writes itself when the business has **recurring revenue, clean retention cohorts, low customer concentration, strong gross margin, defensible technical depth, and a logical platform buyer map**. In this vertical, boring and mission-critical often beats flashy.
