## Answer

**Thesis:** Manufacturing & industrial B2B software is a large, still-fragmented vertical software market where consolidation is accelerating. The best assets are boring in the good way: workflow-critical, deeply embedded in plant operations, tied to compliance or production uptime, and serving a narrow industrial niche with recurring revenue and low churn.

**TAM and growth.** Depending on scope, the market ranges from a narrow industrial-software TAM of roughly **$20B-$60B** to a broader industrial software ecosystem of **$100B+ today**. IoT Analytics estimates global industrial software at **$146B in 2023**, growing to **$355B by 2030**, a **13.5% CAGR**. More narrowly, Manufacturing Operations Management software was **$21B in 2025** and is forecast to reach **$110B by 2034**, a **20.3% CAGR**. The growth drivers are clear: cloud migration, MES/MOM modernization, reshoring, labor scarcity, AI-enabled analytics, IT/OT convergence, regulatory traceability, and supply-chain resilience.

**Fragmentation vs consolidation.** The sector is fragmented at the operating-workflow level and consolidating at the platform-capital level. ERP, MES, QMS, CMMS/EAM, CPQ, CAD/CAM, SCADA, industrial IoT, product data, field service, and supply-chain tools are often purchased by different buyer personas. Geography, language, tax/accounting localization, discrete vs process manufacturing, and compliance specialization keep the long tail alive. IoT Analytics notes that MOM is especially fragmented, with no vendor holding double-digit share. But consolidation is very real: strategic buyers want connected digital-thread platforms, while PE and VMS roll-ups want sticky recurring revenue in niche industrial workflows.

**Most active buyers and consolidators.**

- **Vertical-software roll-ups:** Constellation Software and its groups, including Vela, Volaris, Jonas, Harris, Topicus; Valsoft; Main Capital; Forterro; Aptean.
- **PE-backed industrial software platforms:** Aptean backed by TA, Insight, Charlesbank, Clearlake; Epicor backed by CD&R; IFS backed by Hg/TA/EQT; Forterro backed by Partners Group; Velotic backed by TPG.
- **Strategics:** Siemens, Emerson/AspenTech, Schneider/AVEVA, Rockwell/Plex, PTC, Autodesk, Dassault Systemes, Hexagon, Bentley, Synopsys/Ansys, Trimble, Honeywell.
- **PE sponsors active around the space:** TPG, Partners Group, TA Associates, Insight Partners, Charlesbank, Clearlake, CD&R, Hg, EQT, Thoma Bravo, Vista, KKR, Battery, Main Capital.

**Typical acquisition multiples.** For founder-owned lower-middle-market vertical SaaS, recent benchmarks point to roughly **3.0x-6.5x ARR / revenue** for ordinary quality, with top-quartile deals **8x+ revenue**. Aventis and SEG-linked market data show private SaaS deal medians around **4x-5x EV/revenue** in 2025-2026. For slower-growth but profitable industrial software, especially ERP/MES/QMS/CMMS with services or on-prem mix, buyers often underwrite on EBITDA: high-quality mid-market SaaS commonly lands around **8x-15x EBITDA**, while scaled, high-retention, profitable SaaS can trade materially higher. Premiums go to high gross retention, low services dependency, clean SaaS metrics, mission-critical workflows, proprietary data, and credible AI/automation use cases.

**Recent notable M&A, 2023-2026.**

- **Siemens acquired Altair** in 2025 for about **$10B EV**, strengthening simulation, HPC, AI, and industrial design software.
- **Emerson moved to buy the remaining AspenTech stake** in 2025, valuing AspenTech at about **$16.8B EV**.
- **Synopsys completed its Ansys acquisition** in 2025, one of the largest engineering-simulation software deals.
- **IFS acquired Copperleaf** in 2024 for about **C$1B**, adding asset-investment planning for utilities and infrastructure.
- **Aptean acquired Logility** in 2025 for about **$490M EV**, deepening supply-chain planning software for manufacturing and distribution customers.
- **TPG carved out GE Vernova's Proficy** manufacturing software business for **$600M** and combined it with **PTC Kepware and ThingWorx** into Velotic in 2026.
- **PTC acquired ServiceMax** for **$1.46B**, adding product-centric field service software.
- **Autodesk acquired FlexSim** in 2023, adding factory simulation and operational-efficiency analysis.
- **Rockwell acquired Clearpath Robotics / OTTO Motors** in 2023, adjacent to factory automation and autonomous material handling.
- **Forterro acquired Orgadata** and expanded further into windows, doors, facade, and manufacturing ERP niches.

**Underserved niches and whitespace.**

- Vertical ERP for subsegments still stuck on generic ERP plus spreadsheets: metal fabrication, plastics, contract manufacturing, EMS, food processors, packaging, specialty chemicals, industrial distributors, job shops.
- Lightweight MES/MOM for SMB plants that cannot absorb Siemens/Dassault/Rockwell-style enterprise deployments.
- Quality, traceability, genealogy, and compliance workflows for regulated smaller manufacturers.
- Quoting, costing, CPQ, and engineering-to-order workflows for complex industrial products.
- Maintenance, spare parts, warranty, aftermarket service, and installed-base monetization.
- Supplier portals and customer portals for mid-market manufacturers.
- Industrial data plumbing: OT connectors, historians, edge analytics, data normalization, and plant-to-ERP integrations.
- AI copilots that actually sit inside industrial workflows: quote generation, work instructions, maintenance triage, root-cause analysis, production scheduling, and compliance documentation.

**What makes a durable founder-owned niche leader.** The attractive profile is a company with a narrow ICP, irreplaceable domain workflows, 90%+ gross retention, strong maintenance/SaaS renewal base, pricing power, implementation know-how, and customer relationships built over years. The best ones own a system of record or system of execution, not a dashboard. They have modest but steady growth, high gross margins, low customer concentration, referenceable customers, clean financials, and a product roadmap that moves legacy users to cloud without breaking trust. In this market, "small but indispensable" can be better than "large but generic."
