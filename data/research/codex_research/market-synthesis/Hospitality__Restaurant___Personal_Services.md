## Answer

**Bottom line:** this is an attractive but messy vertical-software market: large enough to support multiple platform companies, still fragmented at the customer and vendor level, and increasingly consolidated by payments platforms, hospitality clouds, short-term-rental platforms, and buy-and-hold vertical-software acquirers.

**TAM and growth.** A practical global "core software" TAM for hospitality, restaurants, and personal services is roughly **$12B-$15B today**, before adding large adjacent revenue pools such as embedded payments, payroll, lending, delivery/order marketplaces, events, and channel-management fees. Public market reports vary by definition, but useful anchors are:

- **Restaurant management software:** about **$6.5B in 2025**, growing to **$14.7B by 2031**, or **14.5% CAGR**, per Mordor Intelligence.
- **Hotel/hospitality management software:** about **$4.2B in 2025**, growing to **$8.4B by 2033**, or **9.8% CAGR**, per Grand View Research.
- **Spa/salon software:** about **$1.0B in 2025**, growing to **$1.9B by 2031**, or **10.7% CAGR**, per Mordor Intelligence.

**Fragmentation vs. consolidation.** The end markets are highly fragmented: independent restaurants, boutique hotels, salons, spas, medspas, franchise groups, and local service businesses still run on mixed stacks of POS, booking, CRM, payroll, messaging, spreadsheets, and legacy desktop tools. Vendor concentration is only moderate in many categories, but capital ownership is consolidating quickly. The winners are trying to own the operating system, guest/customer record, payments flow, and marketing loop.

**Most active consolidators and buyers.**

- **Strategic/platform buyers:** DoorDash, Shift4, Agilysys, Mews, Guesty, Oracle Hospitality, Amadeus, Lightspeed, Toast, Block/Square, and Cloudbeds-style hospitality platforms.
- **Payments-led buyers:** Shift4 is the clearest example; its Revel/Vectron deals were explicitly about expanding POS footprints and payment volume.
- **Buy-and-hold vertical software groups:** Constellation Software units such as **Volaris** and **Jonas/Vertus**, plus **Valsoft/Aspire**, are natural buyers for profitable niche leaders.
- **PE and growth investors:** Blackstone, Vista, KKR, EQT, PSG, JMI, L Catterton, Temasek, and Affinity have all been active around adjacent hospitality, wellness, fitness, and vertical SaaS assets.

**Recent notable M&A / consolidation.**

- **DoorDash acquired SevenRooms** in 2025 for **$1.2B**, adding restaurant CRM, reservations, waitlist, and guest-experience tools.
- **Shift4 acquired Revel Systems** and a majority stake in **Vectron** in 2024, adding 18,000+ Revel merchant locations and ~65,000 Vectron POS locations.
- **Agilysys acquired Book4Time** in 2024 for about **$150M**, expanding into spa and ancillary revenue management for hotels/resorts.
- **Mews acquired Frontdesk Anywhere** in 2024 and Clarity in 2025, continuing a hospitality PMS roll-up strategy.
- **Guesty acquired Rentals United** in 2024 after earlier acquisitions of StaySense, Kigo, HiRUM, and YieldPlanet.
- **Volaris acquired Squirrel Systems** in 2024 and Bit Soft in 2025, expanding its foodservice/hospitality portfolio.
- **Valsoft acquired MonkeyMedia** in 2025, entering restaurant technology.
- **Blackstone acquired Cvent** in 2023 for **$4.6B**, a large adjacent events/hospitality software transaction.
- **Playlist/Mindbody/ClassPass and EGYM** completed a 2026 merger valuing the combined wellness/fitness operating system at **$7.5B**.

**Typical valuation multiples.** For lower-middle-market SMB vertical software in this segment, I'd underwrite in bands rather than one number:

- **Subscale, low-growth, legacy/on-prem:** ~**1x-3x revenue** or **6x-10x EBITDA**.
- **Healthy niche SaaS, profitable, $2M-$20M ARR, 10%-25% growth:** ~**3x-6.5x ARR/revenue** or **12x-20x EBITDA**.
- **Premium strategic asset with payments, strong retention, category leadership, or scarce data/workflow position:** **6x-10x+ revenue**; strategic buyers may pay more when they can monetize payment volume, delivery, marketplace demand, or cross-sell.
- Current SaaS market context is more disciplined: SEG reported median SaaS M&A/private valuation around **4.0x EV/TTM revenue in 2Q26**, while public SaaS compressed to about **3.2x EV/TTM revenue**.

**Underserved niches and whitespace.**

- AI receptionist / missed-call capture for restaurants, salons, spas, medspas, and boutique hotels.
- Unified guest/customer data, loyalty, and direct-marketing tools that let SMBs reduce dependence on marketplaces.
- Medspa-specific workflows: consent, before/after photos, memberships, inventory, provider utilization, compliance, financing.
- Labor scheduling, tip pooling, payroll, training, and retention tools built for hourly hospitality/service teams.
- Back-office automation: inventory, vendor ordering, AP, recipe/menu costing, procurement, and franchise reporting.
- Revenue optimization outside the core booking: spa, golf, events, memberships, packages, upgrades, and dynamic pricing.
- Integration middleware for messy SMB stacks: POS, PMS, accounting, delivery, Google/Meta/TikTok, CRM, payroll, and payments.
- Multi-location "emerging chain" tools between single-store SMB software and enterprise systems.

**What makes a durable, profitable founder-owned niche leader.** The best assets are not generic booking tools. They are systems of record for a painful vertical workflow, with high daily usage, low churn, strong implementation know-how, and a wedge into payments or transaction volume. A durable founder-owned leader usually has a narrow ICP, deep workflow fit, clean data ownership, a reputation for support, 90%+ gross retention, expansion from modules/payments/locations, 70%+ gross margin, and enough profitability to avoid forced fundraising. The nicest profile is boring in the best way: mission-critical, loved by operators, hard to rip out, and too vertical for horizontal SaaS giants to serve well.
