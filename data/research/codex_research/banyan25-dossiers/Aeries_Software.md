## Answer

### Snapshot

Aeries Software is a vertical K-12 EdTech company built around Aeries SIS, a student information system used as the operational system of record for attendance, grades, scheduling, enrollment, state reporting, parent/student portals, analytics, communications, payments, and related workflows. The company is Orange, California-based, founded in 1995, and historically Lloyd-family owned and operated.

### Business Model & Product

Aeries sells mission-critical SIS software and adjacent modules to school districts/LEAs, mainly through district contracts with recurring license/subscription, cloud hosting, implementation, training, support, and add-on product revenue. Its platform includes Aeries SIS, Online Enrollment, Analytics, Communications powered by ParentSquare, Payments, Enroll Path, JumpRope standards-based grading, and ActivityHero for expanded/after-school learning.

This is a sticky vertical SaaS/system-of-record model: SIS replacement is painful because districts must migrate student records, retrain staff, rebuild integrations, and remain compliant with state reporting deadlines.

### Vertical Niche & Market Position

Aeries is strongly positioned in California K-12 SIS. The company states that more than 750 districts and LEAs across California and Texas rely on Aeries, and that “1 in 2” California students use Aeries. LinkedIn says it has implemented Aeries SIS in over 750 California public school districts and education agencies and calls it California’s most popular SIS.

Its market position appears strongest in California, with Texas as a growth vector. This is attractive but also creates geographic concentration risk.

### Revenue / ARR & Profitability Signals

No audited revenue or EBITDA is public. Public third-party estimates vary widely:

- Owler estimates annual revenue at `$5M-$25M`.
- ZoomInfo lists Aeries at `$94.3M` revenue and `51-200` employees in a directory category.
- Growjo search snippets imply about `101` employees and `$145K` revenue per employee, or roughly `$14.6M` revenue.

My M&A underwriting view: treat `$20M-$50M ARR/revenue` as a reasonable preliminary range, with upside if ZoomInfo’s figure is directionally accurate and downside if much of the 750+ district base is smaller LEAs or legacy/on-prem contracts. The company’s 30-year bootstrapped/family-owned history, large installed base, and ability to attract Francisco Partners capital are positive profitability signals, but Dubb’s sell-side case study also notes the bootstrapped model had “financial limitations,” suggesting growth investment was needed for modernization and M&A.

### Employees & Growth Trend

Employee count appears to be roughly `100-200`.

- LinkedIn lists `51-200` employees and shows `128` employee profiles.
- Dubb’s case study lists company size as `100-200`.
- Growjo shows `101` employees.

Trend looks modest-to-positive rather than hypergrowth: the base business is mature, but the Francisco Partners investment and ActivityHero acquisition indicate a new inorganic/product expansion phase.

### Ownership & Funding History

Aeries was founded by the Lloyd family in 1995; LinkedIn names Jerry D. Lloyd as founder. It operated for roughly 30 years as an independent, family-owned and family-operated company.

In January 2025, Aeries announced a growth investment from Francisco Partners. Aeries said it would remain family-operated, while Barry Lloyd and Rich Lloyd retired. Dubb Value Creation’s case study says Aeries sold a majority stake to a financial buyer / PE partner, aligning with Francisco Partners as the likely majority owner. No prior VC equity rounds were found publicly. A 2020 PPP loan appears in public databases, but that should not be treated as venture funding.

### Key Competitors

Core SIS competitors:

- PowerSchool SIS
- Infinite Campus
- Skyward Student Management
- Edupoint Synergy SIS
- Aequitas Q SIS, especially in California
- Focus School Software
- Tyler SIS / other regional public-sector SIS vendors

Adjacent module competitors include ParentSquare, SchoolMint, Eleyo, 6crickets, Sawyer, GoGuardian-adjacent analytics/engagement tools, and specialized standards-based grading products.

### Moat / Defensibility

Aeries’ moat is real, but mostly vertical-market and workflow-driven rather than pure technology monopoly.

Key defensibility factors:

- Deep California state-reporting expertise, especially CALPADS, plus emerging Texas PEIMS/TSDS support.
- High switching costs due to student records, staff workflows, gradebooks, portals, integrations, and compliance.
- 30-year brand trust in California district operations.
- Dense user community via AeriesCon, user groups, Aeries Ideas, and support knowledge base.
- Installed-base expansion opportunity through Analytics, Enrollment, Payments, JumpRope, ActivityHero, training, and cloud hosting.

### Notable Risks

- Geographic concentration: California is a strength and a ceiling unless Texas expansion works.
- Cybersecurity/data privacy: Aeries settled a student-data breach lawsuit for `$1.75M`; The Journal reported later court statements that the breach affected databases across 166 districts and exposed PII for roughly 3 million students.
- Modernization / technical debt: Aeries has publicly discussed product modernization, which implies legacy-platform work remains important.
- Competitive pressure: PowerSchool, Infinite Campus, Skyward, and Synergy can bundle broader cloud suites and AI-enabled workflows.
- PE ownership perception: districts may value Aeries’ family-operated culture; outreach and diligence should test whether FP ownership changes customer sentiment.
- Public-sector sales cycles: renewals are sticky, but new-logo sales face slow procurement, budget cycles, and board scrutiny.

Aeries’ own announcement emphasizes remaining family-operated after Francisco Partners’ investment, and Dubb’s case study says the family wanted a partner who would preserve culture, employees, and legacy rather than “tear apart” the business.
