## Answer

### Executive View

Cliniko is a founder-owned, bootstrapped vertical SaaS company serving allied health and small-to-mid-sized healthcare clinics. It looks like a high-quality, profitable, culturally distinctive niche SaaS asset, but also a very hard M&A target: Cliniko publicly says it has **no investors, no exit plan, and is still not keen on outside investment**.

### Business Model & Product

Cliniko sells cloud practice-management software on a monthly subscription, priced by number of practitioners, not by feature bundle. Public pricing is:

- 1 practitioner: **$45/mo**
- 2-5: **$95/mo**
- 6-8: **$145/mo**
- 9-12: **$195/mo**
- 13-25: **$295/mo**
- 26-200: **$395/mo**
- SMS is extra at **$0.10/message**

All subscriptions include every feature, unlimited admin/reception users, unlimited locations, unlimited patients, file storage, free support, frequent updates, backups, and a minimum 2% revenue donation to charity.

Core product modules: scheduling, online booking, health/treatment records, invoices/payments, expenses/taxes, reporting, multi-location management, SMS/email communications, telehealth, forms, data import/export, API and connected apps such as Xero and Mailchimp.

### Vertical Niche & Market Position

Cliniko is tightly positioned around **clinics and allied health practitioners**, rather than hospitals or large enterprise EHR. Its sweet spot appears to be physiotherapy, osteopathy, psychology, podiatry, myotherapy, exercise science, and adjacent private-practice clinics.

Public scale signals are strong:

- Founded in Melbourne/Australia; global remote company.
- Used in **95+ countries**.
- As of July 2026: **65 employees** and **65,000+ health professionals** using Cliniko.
- A September 2026 redesign post says Cliniko supports **100,000+ people worldwide**, likely including staff/admin users as well as practitioners.
- Partnerships/history include professional bodies such as Osteopathy Australia, the UK Chartered Society of Physiotherapy, Australian Psychological Society, and Exercise & Sports Science Australia.

My read: Cliniko is a respected, global SMB allied-health PMS vendor with especially strong Australia/UK/allied-health credibility, not a broad US enterprise-healthcare platform.

### Estimated Revenue / ARR & Profitability Signals

Cliniko does not disclose ARR or audited financials. Public signals suggest meaningful SaaS scale:

- If 65,000 health professionals were all on the solo $45/month tier, that would imply **$35.1M USD ARR**. Actual ARR could be lower because multi-practitioner tiers sharply discount effective per-practitioner pricing, especially for larger clinics.
- Charity disclosures provide another useful triangulation. Cliniko says it donates at least **2% of revenue**, had donated **AU$1.8M by July 2023**, **just shy of AU$2.7M by March 2025**, and **AU$3M by January 2026**. If donations were near 2%, the July 2023 to March 2025 delta implies roughly **AU$27M annualized revenue** during that period. If they donated above 2%, true revenue would be lower.
- Public third-party revenue databases are inconsistent and often obviously stale; I would underwrite a rough current ARR range of **US$15M-$35M**, with **high-teens to mid-$20Ms USD** as a cautious working estimate until verified.

Profitability signals are unusually explicit:

- Cliniko's pricing page says current prices "allow us to run a profitable company."
- It has no outside investors.
- It has grown to 65 employees while maintaining charity donations and low pricing.
- It funds a 30-hour workweek / six-hour workday culture and still continues hiring/product investment.

No public EBITDA or margin data found.

### Moat / Defensibility

Cliniko's moat is not a patent-style moat; it is an execution, trust, data, and culture moat.

Defensibility factors:

- **Workflow/data embeddedness:** calendars, clinical records, forms, templates, invoices, payment history, communications, reports, and files create switching friction.
- **Trust/compliance:** public emphasis on security, backups, encryption, HIPAA, GDPR, PIPEDA, Australian Privacy Principles, region-aware storage, bug bounties, and third-party security review.
- **Brand goodwill:** no-investor positioning, no price increases since 2011, charity donations, carbon neutrality, ethical-business tone.
- **Focused vertical UX:** built from allied-health pain points rather than generic EHR.
- **Low-price scale advantage:** pricing is hard for smaller funded competitors to undercut while matching product breadth/support.
- **API/integration ecosystem:** Xero, Mailchimp, connected apps, public API.

### Notable Risks

- **M&A willingness risk:** likely the biggest issue. Public messaging says no outside investors and no exit plan. A direct acquisition pitch may be dead on arrival unless framed as stewardship, continuity, and values preservation.
- **Pricing risk:** prices have not changed since June 2011. That is great for goodwill but may suppress ARPU. Raising prices could trigger brand/culture backlash.
- **Competitive intensity:** the SMB practice-management category is crowded, with strong regional competitors and emerging AI-enabled front-office tools.
- **Security/regulatory risk:** stores sensitive health information globally; any breach would be high-impact.
- **Product modernization risk:** the 2026 redesign is a major rewrite/architecture overhaul. Positive long term, but rollout risk exists.
- **Enterprise ceiling:** pricing and product are optimized for clinics/allied health, not necessarily large enterprise health systems.
- **Founder/key-person/culture dependence:** the company's public identity is strongly tied to Joel, Liora, and their operating philosophy.
