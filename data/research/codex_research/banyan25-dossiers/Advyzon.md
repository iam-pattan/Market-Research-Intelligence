## Answer

**Advyzon snapshot:** Advyzon is a Chicago-based wealthtech platform founded in 2012 by **Hailin Li, Ph.D., CFA**, formerly SVP/chief architect behind Morningstar Office. The legal operating company is **yHLsoft Inc., d/b/a Advyzon**; its RIA/TAMP arm, **Advyzon Investment Management LLC (AIM)**, is a wholly owned subsidiary. The company targets RIAs, breakaway advisors, broker-dealers, banks/trusts, aggregators, family offices, and UHNW practices.

**Business model & product:** Core revenue appears to be subscription SaaS plus implementation/service revenue for an all-in-one advisor platform: portfolio accounting/management, performance reporting, trading/rebalancing, CRM/workflows, billing, client portal/mobile app, document storage, business intelligence, account aggregation, and now AI/financial planning. AIM adds asset-management/TAMP-style revenue through managed portfolios, model marketplace, outsourced CIO, personalized indexing, trading, tax optimization, and UMA/SMA capabilities. Its pitch is “one platform / one data model” rather than a stitched-together suite.

**Vertical niche & market position:** Advyzon sits in advisor technology / RIA operating systems, competing most directly with **Orion**, **Envestnet Tamarac**, **SS&C Black Diamond**, **Addepar**, **Morningstar Office successor/migration paths**, and in adjacent modules with **eMoney**, **MoneyGuide/Envestnet**, **RightCapital**, **AssetMark**, **Vestmark**, and **Altruist**. It has moved from challenger to credible category leader in some survey-based metrics: the 2026 T3 / Inside Information survey gave Advyzon the highest All-In-One user rating for the ninth year, with **9.50% all-in-one market share**, and strong CRM, reporting, trading/rebalancing, account aggregation, and document-management ratings. The company says it serves **2,700+ firms**; Citi’s 2026 global UMA selection is a major enterprise validation.

**Scale, revenue/ARR & profitability signals:** Financials are private. Public estimates vary sharply: GetLatka estimates **$11.5M 2024 revenue/ARR**, while Growjo estimates about **$21M annual revenue**. Those estimates may be stale or conservative given Advyzon’s claimed **2,700+ firms**, **200+ team members**, and enterprise push. Profitability is not disclosed. Positive signals: limited disclosed funding history, founder-led history, high claimed client retention (**94%+** on Advyzon’s story page), category-leading satisfaction, and a recurring SaaS + AIM fee mix. Negative/unclear signals: rapid hiring/product expansion, AI/planning/TAMP buildout, and enterprise implementations may suppress near-term margins.

**Employees & growth trend:** Advyzon’s own site says **200+ team members**; LinkedIn lists **51-200 employees**; PitchBook/Tracxn/LeadIQ-type databases cluster around **130-133** employees. The direction is clearly up: GetLatka lists ~75 employees as of late 2024, while Advyzon’s 2026 site claims 200+. About **50% of the team is dedicated to engineering/product**, per company/Citi/JMI materials.

**Ownership & funding history:** Advyzon remains privately held and founder-led by Hailin Li. AIM is wholly owned by yHLsoft/Advyzon. Public database/PitchBook snippets list **$1.05M raised** and **JMI Equity** as an investor; JMI’s own portfolio page lists Advyzon, confirming institutional backing. I found no public round announcement with terms, valuation, ownership %, or control rights. Best read: historically founder-led/bootstrapped or lightly funded, now at least JMI-backed; exact cap table is not public.

**Moat / defensibility:** The strongest moat is workflow and data-model depth: Advyzon’s internally built platform avoids the integration debt that larger roll-up competitors often face. Advisor data, billing, performance reporting, CRM workflows, documents, rebalancing, and now AI/planning live in one operating environment, creating switching costs. Other moats: high advisor satisfaction, expanding integrations with Schwab/Apex/iCapital, community via the Advyzon Conference, AIM’s embedded TAMP/model marketplace, and enterprise credibility from Citi. The moat is good but not impregnable; Orion, Envestnet/Tamarac, Black Diamond, Addepar, and Altruist have scale, capital, and distribution.

**Notable risks:**  
- **Competitive intensity:** Wealthtech is crowded and consolidating; incumbents can bundle, discount, or acquire gaps.  
- **Execution risk:** Advyzon is expanding simultaneously into AI, financial planning, enterprise UMA, family office/UHNW, and TAMP/investment management.  
- **Regulatory/compliance risk:** AIM is an SEC-registered adviser; referral fees, model marketplaces, TAMP services, and AI-driven workflows create conflict, disclosure, and supervision obligations.  
- **Data/security risk:** The platform holds sensitive household, portfolio, billing, CRM, documents, and planning data.  
- **Partner dependency:** Custodian and infrastructure relationships with Schwab, Apex, iCapital, Citi, etc. are strengths but also dependencies.  
- **Diligence gap:** Revenue, EBITDA, net retention, customer concentration, churn, implementation backlog, and JMI terms are not public.
