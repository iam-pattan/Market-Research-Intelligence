## Answer

**Global Shop Solutions is a long-running, family-owned manufacturing ERP vendor** based in The Woodlands, Texas. It sells ERP software for small and midsize manufacturers, especially job shops and discrete/mixed-mode manufacturers, covering quote-to-cash workflows: production scheduling, shop-floor data collection, quality, inventory, purchasing, shipping, CRM/order management, BOM/CAD/nesting, accounting, HR/payroll, dashboards and KPIs.

**Business model & product:** ERP license/subscription plus implementation, training and support. The company supports both cloud/SaaS and on-premise deployments. ERP Research describes pricing as all-inclusive, with indicative first-year deployment costs of roughly **$75K-$200K for 10-30 concurrent users**, though the company itself does not publish list pricing. Recent product messaging emphasizes “AI-enhanced ERP,” including Genii capabilities for AP automation, sales order entry, data extraction/migration, natural-language ERP queries, alerts and dashboard/report creation.

**Vertical niche & market position:** Its core niche is manufacturing ERP for SMB manufacturers. The company says it serves customers in **25+ countries and nearly/over 30 industries**, including aerospace, automotive, defense, medical device, metal fabrication, plastics, packaging, oil & gas, electronics, machine shops and job shops. ERP Research positions it as an “all-in-one ERP for small to midsize manufacturers” and says it is best suited to small discrete/job-shop manufacturers rather than larger multi-site enterprises.

**Estimated revenue/ARR & profitability signals:** Global Shop Solutions is private, so revenue is not audited publicly. Public estimates cluster around **$53M-$60M annual revenue/ARR**: ERP Research cites approximately **$53M**, Latka estimates **$54M 2024 revenue**, and Growjo estimates **$60M**. Profitability is not disclosed. Positive signals: the company repeatedly states it is **debt-free**, **family-owned**, and has **no outside investment**, implying disciplined cash generation, but margin/EBITDA still requires diligence.

**Employee count & growth:** Estimates vary but point to a few hundred employees. Latka lists **315 employees in 2026**, up from **300 in 2023**; Growjo lists **331 employees** and **4% employee growth last year**; Tracxn search data lists **344 employees as of June 2026**. Net read: roughly **315-345 employees**, with low-single-digit headcount growth.

**Ownership & funding history:** Founded in **1976 by Dick Alexander**. Current President & CEO is **Dusty Alexander**, Dick’s son. Public sources consistently describe the company as **100% family-owned/family-operated, bootstrapped, debt-free, and with no outside investment**. Public funding databases show **$0 funding/no disclosed rounds** and no VC/PE investors.

**Key competitors:** For its target market: **JobBOSS²/E2 Shop System, Cetec ERP, ProShop, Fulcrum, Katana MRP, MIE Trak Pro**. For larger/midmarket ERP evaluations: **Epicor Kinetic, SYSPRO, Infor CloudSuite Industrial, NetSuite, SAP Business One, Acumatica Manufacturing Edition, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central**.

**Moat/defensibility:** The main moat is vertical specialization plus long implementation switching costs. The company has ~50 years of manufacturing ERP domain knowledge, a broad integrated module suite, embedded workflows tied to shop-floor operations, in-house implementation/support, and customer-specific configuration. Customer operations data, trained users, reports, manufacturing routings, BOMs, cost history and accounting workflows make replacement painful once deployed.

**Notable risks:** Legacy product perception is the biggest diligence item. ERP Research flags dated UI, weaker built-in reporting/BI, legacy COBOL heritage in parts of the system, learning curve, limited scalability beyond ~250 users, smaller partner ecosystem, no native field service module, and basic CRM/ecommerce depth. Strategic risks include cloud-native competitors, AI feature commoditization by larger ERP vendors, family/key-person succession dynamics, and possible reluctance to sell given the company’s public emphasis on independence and legacy.
