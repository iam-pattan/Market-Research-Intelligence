## Answer

**Vagaro snapshot:** Vagaro is a founder-led vertical SaaS and embedded payments platform for beauty, wellness, fitness, and other service businesses. It sells business management software plus monetizes payments, marketplace booking, add-ons, hardware, payroll/embedded finance, and financing products. Company-disclosed scale is meaningful: Vagaro says **100,000+ businesses**, **300,000 professionals**, **$40B+ payments processed**, and **17 years in business**.

**Product / business model:** Core product is scheduling, online booking, calendar/resource management, client records, marketing, memberships/packages/gift cards, inventory, POS, reporting, payroll, forms/SOAP notes, websites, branded apps, and consumer discovery via the Vagaro marketplace. Pricing starts around **$30/month for one location**, with promo pricing shown at **$23.99/month for the first six months**, plus payment processing and paid add-ons. The model is attractive because revenue expands from SaaS subscriptions into payment volume, hardware, financing, payroll, and consumer marketplace traffic.

**Vertical niche / market position:** Vagaro is a specialist operating system for small and mid-sized beauty, wellness, salon/spa, barber, med-spa, fitness, and service professionals. It sits between lightweight schedulers and higher-end enterprise salon/spa platforms. Market position looks strong in SMB/mid-market: Vagaro claims 100k+ businesses, while LinkedIn says 83k+ businesses and 709 associated employees. Its 2025 acquisition of Schedulicity expanded its professional community to **230k-240k+ beauty and wellness professionals**, per Vagaro's announcement.

**Revenue / ARR estimate:** No audited revenue is public. Best M&A read: likely **$100M+ revenue scale**, with a wide plausible range depending on whether estimates include gross payments revenue. Public third-party estimates are inconsistent: Latka estimated **$65.9M annual revenue**, Growjo estimated **$114.9M**, and LeadIQ estimated **$250M-$500M**. A bottom-up sanity check supports at least a meaningful ARR base: 100k businesses at $30-$100/month implies roughly **$36M-$120M+ subscription ARR** before payments, add-ons, financing, payroll, and hardware. Payments could materially lift gross revenue, but net take-rate economics are not public.

**Profitability signals:** Positive signals include bootstrap discipline before outside capital, first institutional funding only in 2018, embedded payments monetization, relatively moderate headcount for claimed customer scale, and growth-equity rather than venture-style burn funding. Negative/unknown: no EBITDA, cash flow, churn, CAC, NRR, gross margin, or payment net take-rate disclosures. International expansion, acquisition integration, and product breadth may consume margin.

**Employees / growth trend:** Public estimates vary. LinkedIn lists **201-500 company size** and **709 people on LinkedIn**; PitchBook search snippets list **489 employees**; Tracxn/LeadIQ snippets are around **725-730 employees** in 2026. Directionally, Vagaro appears to have grown from a few hundred employees into the **500-750** range, but exact active headcount should be diligence-confirmed.

**Ownership / funding history:** Vagaro is **private, founder-led, and bootstrapped for years**. Founder/CEO Fred Helou started the company in 2009. Public funding history:
- **2009-2018:** bootstrapped/founder-led period.
- **Dec. 2018:** **$63M growth-equity round**, first institutional capital, led by **FTV Capital**. FTV's Robert Anderson and Richard Garman joined the board.
- **Nov. 2021:** additional **FTV Capital-led funding**, terms undisclosed, valuing Vagaro at **$1B**. Some third-party databases estimate total funding around **$163M**, implying ~$100M in the 2021 round, but official releases did not disclose the amount.

**Founder / leadership:** Key public leaders include **Fred Helou, Founder & CEO**; **Nishant Dave, President**; **Kerry Melchior, COO**; **Eric Lee, CIO**; and **Armita Rostamian, CFO**. Other public snippets have also listed Charity Hudnall and Nitin Gupta in senior commercial/marketing roles, but current titles should be verified before outreach.

**Competitors:** Direct and adjacent competitors include **Mindbody**, **Booksy**, **Fresha**, **GlossGenius**, **Square Appointments**, **Zenoti**, **Boulevard**, **Phorest**, **Meevo**, **Acuity Scheduling/Squarespace**, and legacy salon/spa POS or booking systems. Schedulicity is now acquired by Vagaro.

**Moat / defensibility:** Strongest moat is workflow depth plus payments attachment. Vagaro is not just appointment booking; it becomes the scheduling, client, staff, payment, marketing, reporting, inventory, payroll, and financing hub. That creates switching friction. Marketplace supply/demand also helps, though less defensible than payments/workflow data. Its low-cost, broad-feature SMB positioning is a real wedge versus both enterprise platforms and newer salon-first apps.

**Risks:** 
- Highly competitive category, with Square, Fresha, Booksy, Mindbody, GlossGenius, and Zenoti pressuring pricing and payments economics.
- SMB churn and sensitivity to salon/fitness/wellness macro cycles.
- Payments concentration and regulatory/compliance risk, including PCI, HIPAA-adjacent workflows, merchant underwriting, chargebacks, and partner dependency.
- Product breadth can become operational complexity.
- Unclear net revenue quality if a large share is payments gross revenue.
- Schedulicity integration risk and customer migration risk.
- Founder-led ownership may mean higher valuation expectations after the $1B 2021 round.

**Recent news, last ~12-18 months:** Vagaro announced the Schedulicity acquisition in Jan. 2025, launched/expanded embedded finance with Liberis and Vagaro Capital, promoted hardware financing in late 2025, and launched a free business-health assessment tool in Nov. 2025. Adyen also highlighted Vagaro's instant payout use case for 80,000+ beauty and wellness businesses.

**Founder outreach hook:** Fred Helou has a strong founder-origin story: he reportedly conceived Vagaro after struggling to book a haircut while traveling in Korea, then bootstrapped the company with a product-led, "solve the operator's pain" mindset. A credible personalization angle: "Your journey from a simple appointment-booking pain point to a full operating system with embedded payments/finance is exactly the kind of vertical SaaS compounding we study."
