## Answer

**ClassLink snapshot for M&A assessment, as of September 13, 2026**

**Business model & product:** ClassLink is a founder-led, privately held K-12 edtech SaaS company focused on education identity, access, rostering, provisioning, and analytics. Its core suite includes LaunchPad SSO, Analytics, OneSync account provisioning, and Roster Server; paid/advanced products include Analytics+, OneData, DataGuard, ThreatScan, Audit Center, AppTrack, Insights, and StateLink. The model appears to be B2B/B2G annual SaaS sold to school districts, states, and education institutions, with districtwide subscriptions and upsell modules rather than usage-only pricing. ClassLink says every subscription includes its core products, while advanced products are additional purchases.

**Vertical niche & market position:** The niche is "IAM for education," sitting between identity/security, SIS data interoperability, edtech app access, and learning analytics. ClassLink claims 25M+ students and staff, 3,000+ school systems, all 50 U.S. states, 42 countries, and 6,000+ SSO app connections. That makes it one of the two most visible U.S. K-12 access/rostering platforms, alongside Clever. Its positioning is more district-paid and suite-oriented than Clever, which is often described as free to districts but monetized through vendors.

**Estimated revenue / ARR & profitability signals:** No audited financials are public. Third-party estimates vary widely: Latka estimates about **$30.9M 2025 revenue/ARR** and ~281 employees; Growjo estimates **$65.6M annual revenue**; LeadIQ places revenue much higher at **$100M-$250M**, which feels less conservative. A reasonable underwriting range from public data is likely **$30M-$70M ARR/revenue**, with upside if state deals and advanced modules are material. Profitability is not disclosed, but signals are favorable: limited outside funding, 26+ years operating history, durable public-sector contracts, and no visible dependency on recent venture financing. Assume profitability must be validated in diligence.

**Employee count & growth:** Public signals vary. LinkedIn lists ClassLink as **51-200 employees**, while Latka/Growjo/Levels-style databases suggest ~280-330. Tracxn reported 207 employees as of late 2024. The practical diligence estimate is **~200-330 employees**, with modest growth and active hiring in product, technical program management, demand generation, and K-12 sales.

**Ownership & funding history:** ClassLink appears **private and founder-led**. Berj Akian founded the company in 1998 and remains Founder & CEO. Public funding history points to one main early equity round: **$3M in 2005 led by Ascend Venture Group**. Tracxn/Crunchbase-style databases also identify Ascend Venture Group as the primary investor; Preqin mentions NJEDA as a current investor/supporter. There is no clear public evidence of a major VC, growth equity, or PE control transaction for the current ClassLink business.

**Leadership:** Publicly listed leadership includes **Berj Akian, Founder & CEO; Stanley Watts, CTO; Haig Berberian, CFO; Lauren Dill, VP Client Services**. Other visible ClassLink figures include **Jerri Kemble**, National Academic Advisor, and product/partnership voices around ClassLink events and education leadership.

**Key competitors:**  
- **Clever**, the most direct K-12 SSO/rostering competitor, acquired by Kahoot! in 2021.  
- **RapidIdentity / Identity Automation**, more identity/security-heavy.  
- **Google Workspace for Education / Microsoft School Data Sync / Entra ID**, especially where schools standardize on hyperscaler ecosystems.  
- **Okta / OneLogin / enterprise IAM vendors**, mainly at larger districts or higher-ed-adjacent accounts.  
- Adjacent SIS/LMS platforms such as PowerSchool, Canvas/Instructure, Schoology, and Infinite Campus can also capture pieces of workflow or data integration.

**Moat / defensibility:** ClassLink's strongest moat is integration density and workflow embeddedness. It connects SIS data, rosters, accounts, SSO, app access, audit trails, and usage analytics across thousands of schools and vendors. Switching costs are meaningful because migrations touch students, teachers, IT admins, vendor integrations, privacy controls, and back-to-school readiness. The 6,000+ app connections, 3,000+ school systems, state-level positioning, partner ecosystem, and administrator community/events create network effects. The security and data privacy angle also increases stickiness as districts face MFA, student-data, and app-vetting pressure.

**Notable risks:**  
- **Budget and procurement cyclicality:** K-12 budgets are public-sector driven and can slow with stimulus rolloffs or state pressure.  
- **Competitive pressure from Clever:** Clever has a large installed base and different monetization economics.  
- **Platform risk:** Google, Microsoft, SIS vendors, or LMS vendors could bundle more identity/rostering features.  
- **Privacy/security exposure:** ClassLink handles identity and student data workflows, so breach risk and compliance scrutiny are high.  
- **Founder/key-person risk:** Berj Akian's long tenure is a strength but also a succession diligence item.  
- **Revenue opacity:** No public audited revenue, retention, gross margin, or EBITDA data.

**Founder outreach personalization hooks:**  
- Lead with the "started by helping one local school" origin story: Akian founded ClassLink in 1998 after helping a local school with its technology plan.  
- Reference the CoSN 2026 Hurley Private Sector Champion Award, which recognized Akian's long service to K-12 edtech leadership.

**Acquisition read:** ClassLink is a high-quality vertical SaaS asset if diligence confirms net revenue retention, gross margin, low churn, and EBITDA. It has clear strategic value to K-12 platforms, SIS/LMS vendors, identity/security players, or PE-backed edtech rollups. The biggest diligence questions are actual ARR, customer concentration, renewal/retention by district size, profitability, contract duration, data/security posture, founder succession, and how defensible its position is against Clever and bundled Microsoft/Google/SIS alternatives.
