## Answer

**Infinite Campus snapshot:** Infinite Campus is a Blaine, Minnesota-based K-12 edtech company founded in 1993 by **Charlie Kratsch**, who remains Founder & CEO. It sells a mission-critical **student information system (SIS)** plus tightly integrated add-ons for LMS-like learning tools, food service, payments, messaging, online registration, analytics, workflow, translations, and state reporting. Its core pitch is "single database / single login / fewer third-party systems."

**Business model & product:** likely subscription SaaS / hosted enterprise software sold to school districts, consortia, state education agencies, and federal customers, with implementation, training, support, and premium product modules layered on. Public product pages emphasize **1,500+ SIS tools**, **13 releases per year**, **300+ in-house developers**, and **99% renewal rate**. Its statewide product, Infinite Campus State Edition, supports real-time data collection and reporting between local education agencies and state agencies.

**Vertical niche & market position:** this is a focused vertical SaaS business in **U.S. public K-12 SIS**, not broad edtech. Infinite Campus says it serves **3,200+ districts**, **11 million students**, **46 states**, and **10 state education agencies**. It appears to be one of the top U.S. K-12 SIS vendors alongside **PowerSchool, Skyward, FACTS SIS, Tyler/Edupoint Synergy, Focus School Software, Aeries, Blackbaud, Veracross, Alma**, and regional/state-specific incumbents. ListEdTech's 2025 SIS market coverage puts PowerSchool near the top and Infinite Campus among the major challengers.

**Revenue / ARR and profitability signals:** Infinite Campus is private, so there are no audited public financials. Public estimates vary widely: **Growjo estimates ~$100.8M annual revenue** and **Latka estimates ~$300M ARR/revenue for 2024**. I would underwrite public revenue as a broad **$100M-$300M range**, with diligence needed through contract databases, state award documents, and customer count by module. Profitability signals are positive but indirect: the company says it is **employee-owned**, **free from outside investors**, has operated for 30+ years, and maintains 99% renewal. That suggests durability and likely self-funded cash generation, but not a confirmed EBITDA margin.

**Employee count & trend:** official company copy says **650+ employees**, including **300+ in-house developers**. LinkedIn lists **501-1,000 employees**. Revelio estimates **661 employees in 2026**, roughly flat/down slightly from 2025 after growth from 2023 to 2024. Net read: a mature company with stable headcount, not hypergrowth.

**Ownership & funding history:** Infinite Campus is **founder-led and employee-owned**. Its own site says employees are company owners and that the company is free from outside investors. Its security page also says it is **not beholden to shareholders or venture capitalists**. Public funding history appears to be **no disclosed VC/PE funding rounds and no named outside investors**; Latka lists **$0 funding**. For M&A, this matters: a buyer is likely dealing with a mission-driven founder/employee-owner culture, not a sponsor-backed process.

**Founder / leadership:** Founder & CEO is **Charlie Kratsch**. Public leadership databases also list **Andre Burgaud, CTO; Brian Page, CFO; Julie Lane, Chief Product Officer; Micheal Rhodes, Chief Customer Officer; David Van Meter, Chief Operating Officer / product leadership; Dan Orlando, Chief Business Development Officer**. Third-party leadership sources should be verified in diligence.

**Moat / defensibility:** Infinite Campus has a strong vertical moat: SIS is a system of record with high switching cost, multi-year public procurement cycles, data migration pain, parent/teacher/admin habituation, state reporting complexity, compliance burden, and integration sprawl. Its strongest differentiator is the combination of district SIS plus **statewide SEA data infrastructure**. The "no acquired SIS products" message is also a smart trust wedge against competitors that have rolled up multiple legacy systems.

**Notable risks:** key risks are student/staff data security, public-sector procurement cyclicality, RFP rebid risk, implementation complexity, integration demands, pressure from PE-backed PowerSchool, UX expectations from newer tools, and founder/succession risk. Employee ownership may make acquisition structurally and culturally harder. The March/June 2026 Salesforce-related data incident is a diligence item even though reports say student databases were not compromised.

**Recent news, last 12-18 months:** the most material recent item is the **2026 Salesforce-related breach**, where attackers reportedly accessed school staff contact/support data and ShinyHunters claimed responsibility; BleepingComputer reported ~137,000 staff accounts affected. On the positive side, Infinite Campus has been in a major rollout period: **North Carolina's statewide transition was fully operational for the 2025-2026 school year**, and **North Dakota published 2025 implementation materials** after selecting Infinite Campus in late 2024.

**Founder outreach personalization hooks:** Kratsch's story is unusually good for founder-led outreach. Public materials say he worked at **Minnesota Educational Computing Consortium**, the group behind Oregon Trail and Number Munchers, skipped college, was "not a good student" despite being valedictorian, sold a prior company in 1993, briefly became a stay-at-home father, then started Infinite Campus after working inside his own former school district to understand student administration. He frames the company around **purpose vs. profit**, long-term product stability, and transforming public K-12 education. Outreach should lead with stewardship, employee ownership, and continuity rather than "roll-up" language.
