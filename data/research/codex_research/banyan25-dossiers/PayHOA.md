## Answer

**PayHOA snapshot:** PayHOA is a Lexington, Kentucky vertical SaaS company for homeowner and condo associations. It targets self-managed HOAs/COAs and community association management companies with an all-in-one portal for dues collection, accounting, reporting, communications, requests/violations, documents, owner portals, websites, voting/surveys, mailroom, vendors/payables, and bookkeeping services. The company says it is trusted by **6,000+ communities** and its About page shows **576,000+ units managed**.

**Business model:** Subscription SaaS plus payments and services. Public pricing runs from **$49/month billed annually** for 0-25 units to **$249/month billed annually** for 401-500 units, and **$0.55/unit/month** for 500+ units with a $275 monthly minimum. It also charges payment/service fees, including **$2.45 ACH**, **3.5% + $0.50 card**, mailed-letter/check fees, bookkeeping from **$199/month**, and tax filing services.

**Vertical niche & market position:** The niche is community association management software, especially the underserved "volunteer board / self-managed HOA" segment. That is attractive because the U.S. has roughly **373,000 community associations**, **78.1M residents**, and **35.2% of U.S. housing** in associations. TechCrunch reported self-managed HOAs are roughly **30%-40%** of community associations. PayHOA's wedge is "HOA-specific QuickBooks + Stripe + portal + communications + workflow," packaged for non-professional board members.

**Revenue / ARR estimate:** PayHOA does not disclose revenue. A reasonable public-data estimate is **~$7M-$12M subscription ARR**, with possible **$10M-$15M+ total revenue run-rate** including payment monetization, bookkeeping, and tax services. Math: 6,000+ communities at roughly $100-$200/month implies $7.2M-$14.4M gross subscription run-rate; 576,000 units at $0.55/unit/month implies a lower-bound ~$3.8M ARR, but many smaller communities pay higher effective per-unit rates. TechCrunch also reported in May 2024 that PayHOA was **profitable / positive EBITDA**, had **70%+ YoY revenue growth**, and had **652,000+ users**. Inc. lists **260% three-year growth** for 2026, reinforcing a strong growth profile.

**Employee count & trend:** Public signals point to a small but scaling team. TechCrunch said **15 employees** in May 2024; the Series A announcement said PayHOA planned to expand headcount by up to **40%** over the next year; Inc. lists company size as **11-50**; PitchBook search snippets show **30 employees**. I would underwrite current headcount as roughly **30-45**, with growth concentrated in product, sales, marketing, support, and customer success.

Broader property-management platforms: **AppFolio, Buildium, Yardi Breeze, Rent Manager**. Newer/smaller self-managed HOA entrants include **KindHOA** and **PropMIS**.

**Moat / defensibility:** PayHOA's moat is practical rather than deeply technical: vertical workflow fit, embedded accounting/payments, accumulated association data, homeowner portal adoption, bank/payment integrations, customer reviews, and a support/onboarding model built for volunteer boards. Switching costs rise once invoices, ledgers, documents, resident contacts, payment history, violations, and communications live in the platform. The "boring vertical SaaS" angle plus profitability is attractive for M&A.

**Key risks:**  
- Competitive pressure from AppFolio/Buildium/Yardi moving downmarket or HOA specialists matching features.
- Price compression from newer self-managed HOA tools with flat/free pricing.
- Payments, ACH/card compliance, fraud, data security, and trust risks.
- Scaling support without eroding the "human help" advantage.
- HOA legal complexity varies by state; product must keep pace with governance, notices, records, and voting rules.
- Volunteer-board churn can create account-level friction.
- Public HOA fee backlash and new regulatory burdens may alter association budgets or workflows.
