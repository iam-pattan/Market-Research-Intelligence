## Answer

**Legora snapshot as of September 2026:** Legora is a Stockholm-founded, venture-backed legal AI platform, formerly **Leya**, selling collaborative/agentic AI software to law firms and in-house legal teams. It is one of the two most visible independent legal-AI scaleups globally, alongside Harvey.

**Business model & product:** Legora sells an enterprise legal AI workspace/aOS for legal research, document review, drafting, due diligence, regulatory monitoring, workflow execution, and collaboration. The product suite includes **Agent**, **Legal Research**, **Tabular Review**, **Workflows**, **Word/Outlook add-ins**, **Portal**, **Monitors**, and **Lists**. Public pricing is not disclosed; FT reported roughly **$100k per firm annually**, while newer commentary suggests some movement toward usage/consumption-based pricing. Core wedge: embed directly into lawyer workflows and trusted sources rather than being a generic chatbot.

**Market niche & position:** Vertical AI for legal services, especially enterprise law firms and corporate legal departments. Public traction signals are very strong: Legora announced **$100M+ ARR in April 2026**, "more than 1,000 customers" across 50 markets, and later press/FT reporting put it around **$150M ARR** and **1,200-1,500 customers**. Harvey appears larger in the U.S. top-law-firm segment, but Legora has become the main European-founded challenger with fast global expansion.

**Revenue / ARR & profitability signals:**  
Legora publicly claimed it crossed **$100M ARR** less than 18 months after general availability. FT/Dealroom/Sacra-type estimates later put ARR around **$150M in Q2 2026**. No reliable public profitability data. Signals point to "growth first": very large funding, rapid hiring, office expansion, acquisitions, and infrastructure spend. Profitability risk centers on AI inference costs, heavy enterprise implementation/customer-success costs, and whether ARR is clean recurring subscription revenue versus usage/run-rate.

**Employee count & growth:** Public numbers vary by source/date, but the direction is clear: Legora grew from **40 to 400+ employees over roughly 12 months** by March-April 2026. FT/Legora LinkedIn reporting later cited **650-700 employees** with a target of **1,500 by year-end 2026**. Current public estimates cluster around **600-700+**.

**Competitors:**  
Primary direct competitor: **Harvey**. Other competitors/substitutes: Thomson Reuters **CoCounsel**, LexisNexis **Lexis+ AI / Protégé**, Google **Gemini Enterprise for Legal**, Microsoft/OpenAI/Anthropic legal workflows, vLex, Spellbook, Luminance, Robin AI, Eudia, Hebbia, GC AI, CASUS, and law-firm-built internal platforms such as Kirkland/Latham/Freshfields initiatives.

**Moat / defensibility:**  
Legora's moat is not foundation-model ownership. It is customer trust, legal workflow depth, integrations, proprietary source/connectors, embedded collaboration, security posture, and distribution into elite firms. Its "build with lawyers" approach, enterprise certifications, source citations, DMS/VDR integrations, and large customer base create switching costs if it becomes part of daily matter workflows. Funding also enables talent acquisition and acquisitions, such as Wexler.

**Notable risks:**  
High valuation relative to revenue; Harvey and incumbents are well funded; Big Law may build in-house; Google/Microsoft/Thomson Reuters/LexisNexis can bundle legal AI into existing platforms; legal AI has accuracy, confidentiality, privilege, governance, and professional-responsibility risks; AI cost structure may pressure margins; and ARR quality in AI startups is under investor scrutiny. A strategic acquirer would need to diligence cohort retention, net revenue retention, gross margin after inference, implementation burden, and real usage by lawyers versus innovation-lab pilots.
