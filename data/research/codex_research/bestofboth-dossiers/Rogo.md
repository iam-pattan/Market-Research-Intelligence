## Answer

**Rogo snapshot:** Rogo is a New York-based, founder-led, VC-backed vertical AI company for finance. It sells an enterprise AI platform and AI agent, **Felix**, to investment banks, private equity firms, hedge funds, asset managers, and now wealth-management teams. The product automates research, financial modeling, deck/memo creation, diligence, data-room analysis, and other high-stakes finance workflows.

**Business model & product:** Rogo appears to monetize primarily through enterprise SaaS, likely per-seat plus bespoke deployment and support. Public reporting describes a per-seat subscription model, but pricing is not officially disclosed. Its product stack is positioned less as “chatbot for bankers” and more as an **agentic workflow platform**: Felix can handle multi-step tasks; Rogo Deal Room centralizes transaction workflows; integrations pull from internal systems, CRM/SharePoint, VDRs, and licensed data sources such as FactSet, Capital IQ, PitchBook, Preqin, LSEG, Dow Jones, Quartr, and Daloopa.

**Vertical niche & market position:** Rogo is in the fast-growing “AI for financial services / AI analyst for Wall Street” niche, with particular strength in investment banking and private markets. It claims deployment across **50,000+ bankers/investors and 350+ institutions** as of September 2026, up from 35,000+ professionals and 250+ institutions reported in April 2026. Named customers/users or public references include Rothschild & Co, Jefferies, Lazard, Moelis, Nomura, Tiger Global, Truist, Baird, and others. That makes Rogo one of the visible category leaders in vertical AI for finance, though it is still competing against much larger incumbents and foundation-model labs.

**Revenue / ARR / profitability signals:** Rogo does not disclose audited revenue, ARR, gross margin, burn, or profitability. Public research cites revenue growth from about **$2M in 2024 to more than $15M in 2025**, and Rogo said in July 2026 that ARR grew **more than 50% quarter-over-quarter** in its largest new-business quarter to date. Given 50,000+ users and large-enterprise buyers, 2026 ARR could be materially above 2025 revenue, but exact ARR is not public. Profitability is unlikely to be the current optimization target: the company raised large growth rounds, is hiring aggressively, opening new offices, acquiring products, and expanding globally.

**Employee count & trend:** Public data points cluster around **150-200 employees** in mid/late 2026. Business Insider reported in August 2026 that Rogo had doubled headcount to **150+** over the prior year and planned to double again; Contrary listed **193 employees** in July 2026. Trend: very rapid growth, with planned expansion in New York, London, San Francisco, Singapore, EMEA, and APAC.

**Ownership & funding history:** Rogo is **private, founder-led, and heavily VC/strategic-backed**. It is not bootstrapped, family-owned, or PE-owned.

Publicly reported funding history:
- **Pre-seed / early financing:** Data-provider snippets indicate an early 2022 round around **$1.5M** involving AlleyCorp / ScOp / BoxGroup, but this is less consistently reported than later rounds.
- **Seed, Feb. 2024:** **$7M**, led by AlleyCorp; other reported investors include Company Ventures, BoxGroup, and ScOp Ventures.
- **Series A, Oct. 2024:** **$18.5M**, led by Khosla Ventures, with AltCapital, Original Capital, Mantis VC, AlleyCorp, BoxGroup, ScOp VC, and insiders reported.
- **Series B, Mar./Apr. 2025:** **$50M**, led by Thrive Capital; J.P. Morgan and Tiger Global reported as participants; valuation reportedly rose to **$350M**.
- **Series C, Jan. 2026:** **$75M**, led by Sequoia Capital, with Henry Kravis, Wells Fargo, Thrive, Khosla, Tiger Global, and J.P. Morgan; total funding >$165M.
- **Series D, Apr. 2026:** **$160M**, led by Kleiner Perkins, with Sequoia, Thrive, Khosla, J.P. Morgan Growth Equity Partners, BoxGroup, Mantis VC, Jack Altman, Evantic, and Positive Sum; total funding >$300M; valuation publicly reported around **$2B**.
- **Strategic investment, Sep. 2026:** roughly **$30M** reported by WSJ; Rogo names Barclays, BNP Paribas / Opera Tech Ventures, Citi Ventures, MUFG Innovation Partners, Société Générale, and Sixth Street co-founder Josh Easterly. Rogo says nine global banks are now on its cap table.

**Founders & leadership:**
- **Gabriel “Gabe” Stengel** — CEO and co-founder; Princeton CS; former Lazard banker / internal AI tooling work.
- **John Willett** — COO and co-founder; Princeton; former Barclays and JPMorgan junior banker; leading London / European expansion.
- **Tumas Rackaitis** — co-founder and founding CTO; software engineering and finance background.
- **Joe Xavier** — CTO, joined July 2026; formerly CTO of Grammarly, with prior roles at Amazon, Twitter, and Microsoft.
- **Rahul Rekhi** — President, reportedly joined Oct. 2025; former Lazard MD and U.S. Treasury counselor.

**Key competitors:**
- **Direct vertical AI / finance workflow:** Hebbia, Model ML, Finster AI, Brightwave, Farsight AI, Mosaic, Mako AI.
- **Market-intelligence incumbents:** AlphaSense, Bloomberg, S&P Global / Kensho, FactSet, PitchBook, Capital IQ ecosystem tools.
- **Foundation-model labs:** OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft Copilot, especially as they add Excel, finance-agent, and enterprise-data integrations.

**Moat / defensibility:** Rogo’s defensibility is strongest in workflow depth, domain specialization, compliance posture, enterprise trust, and strategic distribution. Its team includes ex-bankers and engineers; it integrates into tools bankers already use; it has bank investors and named institutional customers; and its deployments likely create switching costs through internal data, permissions, workflows, and institutional-memory layers. Its acquisitions of Subset, Rivanna, Arvo, and Offset also suggest a roll-up strategy around adjacent finance-agent workflows.

**Moat limitations:** Rogo does not appear to own a unique proprietary financial-data corpus at the scale of Bloomberg or AlphaSense. It relies partly on third-party licensed data and frontier models. If OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, Bloomberg, or S&P/Kensho build comparable agentic workflows directly into Excel, terminals, or enterprise systems, Rogo’s application-layer margin could be pressured.

**Notable risks for M&A diligence:**
- **Valuation risk:** $2B valuation on reported 2025 revenue >$15M implies a very high revenue multiple unless 2026 ARR has scaled dramatically.
- **Customer concentration risk:** Large banks can be high-ACV but slow, security-heavy, and politically complex buyers.
- **Platform dependency:** OpenAI/Anthropic/Microsoft may be both suppliers and competitors.
- **Data moat risk:** Third-party data integrations are useful but not exclusive.
- **Regulatory / InfoSec risk:** The product handles sensitive deal, client, and investment data; any breach, hallucination, or misuse could be severe.
- **Usage-cost risk:** AI inference costs can scale unpredictably; WSJ reported Rogo had to add usage controls after an intern nearly incurred AI-service fees at a $250K annualized rate.
- **Cycle risk:** Investment-banking and private-markets workflows are tied to M&A / capital-markets activity.

**Recent news, last 12-18 months:**
- Apr. 2025: $50M Series B led by Thrive; valuation reportedly $350M.
- Jan. 2026: $75M Series C led by Sequoia; London office announced.
- Apr. 2026: $160M Series D led by Kleiner Perkins; $2B valuation reported.
- Jul. 2026: Joe Xavier joins as CTO; Rogo says ARR grew >50% QoQ.
- Aug. 2026: Rogo launches Deal Room and acquires Rivanna for AI-native due diligence.
- Sep. 2026: Acquires Arvo, partners with Datasite, and announces strategic investments from major global financial institutions.
- Sep. 2026: WSJ reports expansion into wealth management.

**Founder outreach hooks:** Strong personalization angles include: the Princeton senior-thesis origin story around an econometrics chatbot; Stengel and Willett having “lived the problem” as junior bankers; Rogo’s shift from “automating junior work” to being a “thought partner” for senior dealmakers; the strategic bank investor cap table; London expansion led by Willett; and the recent move into wealth management and institutional memory via Deal Room, Arvo, Datasite, and Rivanna.
