## Answer

**Company snapshot.** EvenUp is a VC-backed, privately held legal AI company founded in 2019 and headquartered in San Francisco. It sells a "Claims Intelligence Platform" for plaintiff-side personal injury firms, covering intake, treatment, demand packages, negotiation, discovery, trial prep, analytics, and outsourced pre-litigation support.

**Business model & product.** EvenUp appears to monetize through firm subscriptions, per-case pricing, and service-heavy workflows. Public product pages reference "all-in-one, case-based pricing," AI-generated and attorney-reviewed demand packages, Microsoft Word drafting, medical chronologies, settlement benchmarking, communication agents, and PLAAS, an outsourced "Pre-Litigation as a Service" offering. The wedge was demand-package automation; the roadmap has broadened into full case-lifecycle automation.

**Vertical niche & market position.** The niche is personal injury law, especially plaintiff firms with high case volume and heavy medical-record workflows. EvenUp claims 2,000+ PI firm customers, including 20% of the top 100 U.S. PI firms, 10,000+ cases processed weekly, 200,000+ resolved cases, and $10B+ in damages secured or claimed. That makes it one of the clear leaders in PI-specific AI, with a narrower but deeper positioning than broad legal AI vendors like Harvey or Legora.

**Revenue / ARR and profitability signals.** No audited revenue or EBITDA figures are public. Directionally, public signals point to high-growth, not mature profitability. EvenUp said ARR doubled year over year in its October 2025 Series E announcement. New Market Pitch summarizes market estimates putting EvenUp around $100M ARR, citing prior 2024 reporting around $35M annualized revenue and expectations of passing $50M. GetLatka separately estimates $110M+ ARR, but that should be treated as third-party directional data. With 500-850+ employees, heavy AI compute, legal-ops review, and a PLAAS services layer, I would underwrite profitability cautiously: likely strong revenue growth, but gross margin may be below pure SaaS and operating margins likely suppressed by hiring/product expansion.

**Employees & growth.** Public counts vary by source. EvenUp announced in July 2025 that it had grown nearly 50% year over year and employed 500+ people. LinkedIn currently shows 501-1,000 employees and 852 associated profiles. PitchBook search snippets show 822; Tracxn/LeadIQ show roughly 849-854 as of July 2026. The clean takeaway: headcount grew from about 100 to 500 by mid-2025 and appears to be roughly 800-850 by mid/late 2026.

**Ownership & funding history.** EvenUp is founder-led and VC-backed, not bootstrapped, family-owned, or PE-controlled from public evidence. Founders are Rami Karabibar, Raymond Mieszaniec, and Saam Mashhad. Public funding history totals about $385M:

**Key competitors.** Direct PI/plaintiff AI competitors include Eve Legal, Supio, ProPlaintiff.ai, Tavrn, TrialBase, Precedent, Anytime AI, and newer open-source or lightweight demand-letter tools. Adjacent competitors include Filevine, Litify, CASEpeer, SmartAdvocate, Clio, and LexisNexis products if they extend deeper into PI workflows. Broad legal AI platforms like Harvey, Legora, Spellbook, CoCounsel, and Paxton are less PI-specialized but relevant if they move downmarket or become workflow platforms.

**Moat / defensibility.** EvenUp's strongest moat is vertical data and workflow depth: it says Piai is trained on hundreds of thousands of injury cases and millions of medical records, plus a settlement repository of 250,000+ verdicts/settlements. Other defensibility factors: 2,000+ firm footprint, PI-specific integrations, attorney/human-review workflows, brand credibility, and channel validation from LexisNexis/RELX investment and alliance. The moat is real but not invulnerable; PI case data, workflow adoption, and trust matter more than generic LLM capability.

**Notable risks.** Key diligence risks are: valuation risk at $2B+ versus uncertain public ARR; margin risk from human review and PLAAS services; legal/ethical risk if AI outputs are wrong or insufficiently supervised; privacy/security exposure because PI matters include medical records and PHI; customer adoption risk if firms resist workflow change; competitive risk from PI-native startups and incumbents like Filevine/Clio/LexisNexis; and model dependency/cost risk. Public Glassdoor sentiment is mixed, so culture and attrition should be checked in management calls.

**Recent news, last 12-18 months.** In May 2025, EvenUp launched AI Drafts Suite, Smart Workflows, Medical Bills Summary, and per-case pricing. In July 2025, it opened a new San Francisco HQ and disclosed 500+ employees and 1,500+ firms. In October 2025, it raised a $150M Series E at a $2B+ valuation. In December 2025, it announced Medical Management and AI Communication Agents. In September 2026, LexisNexis and EvenUp announced a strategic alliance for PI professionals.

**Leadership.** Public founder/leadership names: Rami Karabibar, CEO and co-founder; Raymond "Ray" Mieszaniec, COO and co-founder; Saam Mashhad, Chief of Product & Legal Operations and co-founder. Saam is publicly described as a former litigation attorney; Bessemer and TechTO both highlight Ray's family experience with a catastrophic accident as part of the company's origin story.

**Founder outreach hooks.** Strong public personalization angles:

- Congratulate the team on the LexisNexis strategic alliance and REV/RELX joining the Series E, since that is unusually relevant distribution/credibility for legal AI.
- Reference the original "level the playing field" mission: Ray's family experience after a catastrophic accident and Saam's litigation background make the story more personal than generic AI automation.
- Frame the acquisition conversation around building the "claims intelligence layer" for plaintiff PI, not just demand drafting.
- Mention the SF/Toronto hub shift and rapid headcount growth as a scaling inflection point where strategic infrastructure, distribution, compliance, and enterprise partnerships may matter more.

**M&A read.** EvenUp is probably too expensive for a traditional tuck-in unless the buyer has a strategic thesis around owning PI claims infrastructure. The best acquirers would be legal data/platform companies, case-management incumbents, litigation-finance/claims ecosystem players, or information-services companies that value the dataset and workflow distribution. The asset is attractive; the key diligence question is whether ARR quality, retention, gross margin, and data rights justify the $2B+ private valuation.
