## Answer

**M&A read:** Infinite Campus is a founder-led, employee-owned K-12 student information system (SIS) vendor with strong strategic value, but likely low seller motivation. Its own positioning is explicitly anti-outside-investor: 650+ employee-owners, “free from outside investors,” focused on innovation/service over profit pressure. That is a cultural moat and a deal hurdle.

**Business model & product**
- Sells mission-critical SIS software to K-12 districts, charters, consortia, state education agencies, and federal education customers.
- Core product is an all-in-one SIS with 1,500+ tools for enrollment, attendance, grades, scheduling, parent/student portals, records, reporting, and communication.
- Add-ons expand wallet share: Food Service, Messenger, Online Registration, Payments, Analytics, Data Suite, Learning Suite, Workflow, report translation, implementation, training, technical services, and support.
- The strongest product angle is “single database / single login / fewer third-party systems,” especially valuable for statewide data/reporting.

**Vertical niche & market position**
- Niche: U.S. public K-12 SIS and statewide K-12 data/reporting modernization.
- Scale: public sources cite 3,200+ districts, 11M students, 46 states, and 10 state education agencies.
- Market share: ListEdTech/Info-Tech put Infinite Campus at roughly 10% of identified K-12 SIS implementations, behind PowerSchool and FACTS SIS, and ahead of Skyward.
- Positioning: one of the few scaled SIS vendors that grew organically rather than via roll-up acquisition. That contrasts with PowerSchool’s acquisition-heavy path and Bain’s 2024 take-private of PowerSchool.

**Revenue / ARR and profitability signals**
- Private-company estimates conflict materially:
  - Growjo: estimated annual revenue of ~$100.8M and 576 employees.
  - Latka: estimated 2024 revenue/ARR of ~$300M, $0 funding, 617 employees.
  - Public workforce data and the company’s own site point to ~650+ employees.
- Practical underwriting view: treat public revenue as a wide range, likely low-to-mid nine figures, with $300M as an aggressive claim needing validation.
- Profitability signals are positive but indirect: 30+ years operating without outside capital, 99%+ renewal claims, employee ownership, 300+ in-house developers, major headquarters investment, and large statewide deployments. The company likely reinvests heavily rather than optimizing EBITDA.

**Employees & growth trend**
- Company says 650+ employees and 300+ in-house developers.
- Revelio estimates 661 employees as of March 2026, up 15.7% from 2023 but down 1.1% YoY in 2026. Engineering is estimated at ~50% of headcount.
- Signal: mature scaled company, modest recent hiring cooling, not hypergrowth.

**Ownership & funding history**
- Founder: Charlie Kratsch founded Infinite Campus in 1993 and remains Founder & CEO.
- Ownership: company describes itself as employee-owned and free from outside investors. A 2019 Star Tribune profile described Kratsch as CEO and majority shareholder.
- Funding history: no public VC/PE funding found. Latka lists $0 funding; Infinite Campus itself says it grew without outside investment. Publicly disclosed investors: none.

**Leadership**
- Charlie Kratsch, Founder & CEO.
- Publicly listed executives include Brian Page, CFO; Andre Burgaud, CTO; Micheal Rhodes, Chief Customer Officer; Dan Orlando, Chief Business Development Officer; Julie Lane, Chief Product Officer; David Van Meter, CPO/COO roles depending on source.

**Key competitors**
- PowerSchool, Skyward, FACTS SIS, Edupoint Synergy, Blackbaud, Frontline/eSchoolPlus, Aeries, Focus, Tyler legacy SIS customers now migrating via partnership, and regional/state-specific SIS vendors.
- PowerSchool is the strategic benchmark: larger, PE-backed, acquisitive, and recently subject to major security scrutiny.

**Moat / defensibility**
- Deep switching costs: SIS is the system of record for students, attendance, grading, state reporting, portals, and integrations.
- State reporting complexity: local compliance and data standards create sticky vendor knowledge.
- Statewide deployments: 10 SEAs create reference credibility that smaller vendors struggle to match.
- Product breadth: add-ons reduce third-party sprawl and increase expansion revenue.
- Cultural moat: founder-led, employee-owned, long product continuity, no acquired-product end-of-life history.
- Retention: company claims 99%+ district renewal.

**Notable risks**
- Cyber/data risk: March 2026 Salesforce/ticketing breach tied to ShinyHunters; HIBP later listed 137k affected email addresses with names, phone numbers, addresses, support tickets, etc. Infinite Campus said core student databases were not accessed.
- FERPA / student privacy sensitivity: even vendor-support data can create district reputational risk.
- Procurement cyclicality: long public-sector RFP cycles; large wins can be lumpy.
- Mature market: SIS is replacement-driven, not greenfield.
- Founder/mission risk: any acquirer perceived as PE-style profit pressure may face employee/customer resistance.
- Customer concentration: statewide deployments are valuable but increase implementation and political accountability risk.
- Enrollment/funding pressures: K-12 budgets are exposed to public funding shifts and declining enrollment in some regions.

**Recent news, last 12-18 months**
- March 2026: Infinite Campus disclosed unauthorized access to an employee Salesforce account; public reporting tied the incident to ShinyHunters. Company said core student databases were not accessed.
- June 2026: Have I Been Pwned added the Infinite Campus breach, listing 137.1k affected addresses.
- 2026 workforce signal: Revelio showed headcount roughly flat/down YoY after growth from 2023.
- Important but slightly older sales momentum: North Dakota selected Infinite Campus in November 2024 as its statewide SIS/data modernization vendor, becoming the tenth SEA; North Carolina selected Infinite Campus in November 2023.

**Personalization hooks for founder outreach**
- Charlie Kratsch’s official founder story is unusually rich: he bypassed college, worked around the MECC/Oregon Trail education-software ecosystem, sold a prior company in 1993, briefly retired as a stay-at-home father, then returned to education tech to “make a difference, not money.”
- He served as technology director at his former district to deeply learn school operations before building the SIS.
- Infinite Campus calls its headquarters the “mothership,” inspired by Star Trek’s Enterprise.
- Outreach should not lead with “growth capital” or “margin expansion.” Better angle: stewardship, employee-owner liquidity, preserving independence, protecting the mission, and helping the next 25 years of K-12 modernization.
