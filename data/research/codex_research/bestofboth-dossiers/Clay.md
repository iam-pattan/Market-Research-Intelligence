## Answer

**Clay snapshot:** Clay Labs Inc. is a New York-based, VC-backed AI go-to-market platform founded in 2017. It started as a broad no-code/self-filling spreadsheet concept and narrowed into GTM data enrichment, workflow automation, AI research agents, and outbound/pipeline systems for revenue teams.

**Product & business model:** Clay sells a GTM “development environment” for growth/revenue teams. Core modules include data marketplace/enrichment, signals, workflows, AI agents/Claygent, CRM enrichment, TAM sourcing, outbound, ABM, ads audiences, sequencer, and rep/account research. The model appears to be SaaS subscription plus usage/data credits and enterprise packages, with Clay acting as a unified buying/orchestration layer across 150-200+ data and AI providers.

**Market position:** Clay is positioned in a fast-growing niche between sales intelligence, RevOps automation, AI SDR/outbound, and data enrichment. Its strongest wedge is “GTM engineering”: technical RevOps/growth operators building repeatable revenue systems instead of manually prospecting. Clay says it serves 17k+ customers, including 80% of the Forbes AI 50 and companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Stripe, ElevenLabs, Visa, Siemens, and UPS. In August 2025, it reported 10k+ customers and 150+ data sources.

**Revenue/ARR & profitability signals:** Clay publicly announced crossing **$100M ARR on Dec. 8, 2025**, up from $1M ARR in two years. In September 2026 it said 2025 revenue grew 4x. No audited profitability, EBITDA, gross margin, or cash burn figures are public. Positive signals: Clay claims zero enterprise churn, enterprise NRR over 200%, and that each dollar invested grows Clay “roughly 15x.” M&A diligence should treat current ARR as **at least $100M+**, but request 2026 ARR, gross margin after data/LLM costs, credit liability, net expansion by cohort, and burn/profitability.

**Employee count & growth:** Public headcount data is inconsistent. Third-party/public commentary around 2025-2026 points to roughly **~175-250 employees**, while Clay’s own careers page shows a broad named team and offices in NYC, SF, and London. Trend is clearly expansionary: recent funding rounds, new offices, active hiring, and enterprise product launches all indicate rapid scaling.

**Ownership & funding:** Clay is founder-led and venture-backed, not bootstrapped, PE-owned, or family-owned. Public funding history:

Disclosed funding is roughly **$319M+**, depending on rounding/undisclosed pre-seed treatment.

**Founder/leadership:** Key public leaders are **Kareem Amin**, co-founder/CEO; **Varun Anand**, co-founder; and earlier co-founder **Nicolae Rusan** is listed by First Round. CapitalG says Amin founded Clay in 2017 and Anand joined as co-founder in 2021. Public leadership hooks include Osman Sheikhnureldin as Head of GTM Engineering and Everett Berry in GTM/sales leadership content.

**Competitors:** Clay competes with several overlapping categories:

- Sales intelligence/data: ZoomInfo, Apollo, Cognism, Lusha, LeadIQ, Clearbit/HubSpot Breeze, FullEnrich.
- GTM orchestration/RevOps/intent: Demandbase, 6sense, Common Room, Unify, Cargo.
- Outbound/AI SDR/sequencing: Salesloft, Outreach, Amplemarket, Instantly, Smartlead, Lemlist, Persana, La Growth Machine.
- Automation/no-code/agent workflows: Gumloop, PhantomBuster, Zapier-like and custom internal GTM engineering stacks.

**Moat/defensibility:** Clay’s defensibility is not just data ownership. It is the combination of workflow flexibility, hundreds of integrations/providers, AI research agents, templates, community, brand with elite AI-native customers, and the emerging “GTM engineer” category it helped popularize. Its strongest moat may be workflow embeddedness: once Clay becomes the GTM data/action layer across CRM, warehouse, enrichment vendors, intent, email, ads, and reps, replacement can be operationally painful.

**Risks for acquirer diligence:**

- **Valuation risk:** $7.1B is a rich multiple if benchmarked against the last confirmed $100M ARR.
- **Data/privacy/compliance:** Clay processes B2B personal/company data and relies on many providers; GDPR/CCPA, data provenance, consent, DNC, and outbound deliverability should be deeply diligenced.
- **Provider dependency:** Margins and reliability depend on third-party data/AI providers and LLM costs.
- **Pricing/credit friction:** Competitors and agencies frequently position against Clay on complexity and unpredictable credit burn.
- **Competitive compression:** ZoomInfo, Apollo, Demandbase/6sense, HubSpot, Salesforce ecosystem players, and AI SDR startups can copy pieces of the workflow.
- **Category hype:** “GTM engineering” is early; demand may be strongest among tech-forward revenue teams and agencies.
- **M&A integration:** The culture is founder-led, creative, and idiosyncratic. Heavy-handed integration could damage the community and talent moat.

**Recent news, last ~12-18 months:** Series C in August 2025 at $3.1B; $100M ARR announced December 2025; 2026 product expansion into agents, MCP/API/CLI, ads audiences, workflows, and sequencer; September 2026 Series D at $7.1B with 17k+ customers.

**Founder outreach hooks:** Strong public hooks include: congratulate Amin/Anand on the eight-year “overnight success” from broad spreadsheet to GTM infrastructure; reference Varun’s “reverse demo” approach where prospects drive the product live; reference Clay’s creation of the GTM engineer category; or connect to Kareem’s repeated theme of making programming-like leverage accessible to non-technical teams. A thoughtful M&A note should frame around preserving Clay’s category leadership and community, not just distribution or cost synergies.
