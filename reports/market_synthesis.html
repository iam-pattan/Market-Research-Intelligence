Executive summary

The 14 briefs sort cleanly into three bands by fit to Banyan's specific thesis (profitable, founder/family-owned, high-recurring niche leaders, ~$2M-$100M, held forever). BAND 1 — genuine hunting grounds: Education, Manufacturing & Industrial, and Construction & Field Service, followed closely by Healthcare (practice-management layer), Legal/Risk/Compliance (the boring EQMS/CLM/loan-servicing tail, not the AI cohort), Real Estate & Property, and the insurance/long-tail "Other Vertical SaaS." These are regulated, deeply fragmented markets with a consolidated platform tier on top and a large founder-owned sub-$100M (often sub-$20M) tail underneath that the big platforms under-hunt — Banyan's structural lane. BAND 2 — structurally attractive but narrow, thin, or crowded: Fintech (a small genuine cluster of loan-servicing/CU-core/RIA back-office buried under VC noise), Government (good fragmentation but the single most contested corner), Hospitality/Personal Services (density 0.07, payments-strategics disintermediating), Logistics (only ~5-6 genuine of 23). BAND 3 — off-thesis as screened: Security (screened as horizontal cyber = all VC), Dev Tools/Data/AI Infra (density 0, VC-saturated), and Horizontal AI (six VC unicorns). In Band 3, density=0 is a TRUE signal, not a confidence-discount artifact — and the real Banyan lanes (physical-security VMS; boring Postgres/test-data/API-contract infra) sit OUTSIDE the screened cohorts and require re-scoped discovery. Opportunity concentrates where three conditions coincide: a mandated/regulated workflow with a proprietary content/audit-trail moat; a fragmented long tail below a consolidated platform; and VC money looking elsewhere (usually the AI-native adjacency). Education SIS/administration, sub-vertical manufacturing ERP/QMS, specialty-trades FSM, and EQMS satisfy all three best. The dominant competitive fact across every Band 1/2 segment: Banyan is not primarily bidding against Thoma Bravo or the strategics — it is bidding against Constellation Software's operating groups (Volaris/Harris/Vela/Jonas/Topicus) and Valsoft, plus Main Capital/Everfield in Europe, whose permanent-hold thesis is identical. Banyan's edge is entry-multiple discipline (~2-4x revenue / 8-15x EBITDA on proprietary, sub-radar deals), and that edge is being compressed as those rivals reach down-market.

Cross-segment themes

  • Barbell/two-speed structure is universal: every segment has a consolidated platform tier (bid up by strategics and mega-PE) sitting on a fragmented founder-owned long tail below ~$100M. Banyan's lane is consistently that under-hunted sub-$100M (often sub-$20M) tier — never the category leaders.
  • The confidence-discount artifact vs. the VC tell — the two signals that must never be conflated: genuine private founder-owned targets systematically land at tier C because public web can't verify their financials (low/zero verified density in Education, Manufacturing, Healthcare, LRC, Gov is a VERIFICATION artifact, not absence of fit). But a high growth score paired with a mediocre Banyan score = VC-backed non-target — and in the AI-native cohorts (Horizontal AI, Dev Tools/Data/AI Infra, cyber-Security) density=0 is the literal truth, not a discount to be argued away.
  • Regulation + proprietary content is the durable moat that recurs everywhere: the most defensible niches own forms, jurisdictional rule mappings, audit trails, state-reporting logic, compliance checklists — not a thin AI feature. This is what makes boring niche leaders sticky and buy-and-hold-FOREVER-underwritable.
  • Constellation Software (Volaris/Harris/Vela/Jonas/Topicus) and Valsoft appear as competing acquirers in essentially every segment — they, not the PE megafunds or strategics, are Banyan's true competitive set for founder-owned sub-$100M vertical software.
  • 'Looks founder-owned' is not 'is founder-owned' — cap-table verification is mandatory. Cautionary tales recur across briefs: Agiloft (KKR-majority since 2024), ClassLink (JMI), Tripleseat (Vista-majority), PayHOA (2024 Series A conversion), Cogitate (probable growth equity). Every name must clear a funding/ownership check before it counts.
  • Screen contamination is pervasive: names bleed across segments (WorkOS and education/gov-ERP into Manufacturing; Semgrep into Education; Material Security into Hospitality; construction/field-service names into LRC). Every shortlist requires manual de-contamination before use.
  • AI disruption is a double-edged buy-and-hold risk: VC AI-native names (Harvey, Abridge, Sierra, etc.) attack exactly the sticky workflows — CLM, legal research, clinical notes, document review — that make Banyan's boring incumbents durable. Held-forever underwriting must stress-test moat erosion, favoring slow-changing regulated niches over fast-churning ones.
  • Multiple inflation from rising permanent-hold and PE competition is compressing Banyan's entry-discount edge across LRC, Manufacturing, Gov, Real Estate, Construction — the proprietary-deal advantage is the defense, and it is eroding as roll-ups reach down.
  • Genuine targets cluster geographically outside the US VC ecosystem — ANZ/UK (trades FSM, allied-health PM), Canada (property PMS, lending LOS, GRC), Estonia/Greece (manufacturing MRP). Bootstrapped founder-owned leaders concentrate where venture capital is thin, but so do European permanent-hold rivals (Everfield, Main Capital, Topicus).
  • Revenue-quality dilution is a recurring diligence trap: hardware/telematics mix (fleet-GPS), services/implementation-heavy delivery (WMS, e-discovery, public-sector), and license/support-vs-subscription models (some EQMS/ERP) can depress true recurring-SaaS purity — screen for recurring-software share, not blended revenue.

  • All revenue and profitability figures are Codex/Growjo-style web-research estimates at ~0.25-0.55 confidence — directional, not fact; do not underwrite off them.

  • Scale-floor risk: several genuine fits are at or below the ~$2M revenue floor (Total ETO, Megaventory, MRPeasy, Tradify, BuildBook, Buildern, RealtyJuggler), where deal size may be below Banyan's efficient minimum and key-person/customer-concentration risk is high.
  • Founder-non-seller risk on the best assets (Cliniko, TherapyNotes, and the strongest bootstrapped names generally): availability is not the same as fit, and the cleanest profiles are often the least likely to sell.
  • Precedent-M&A multiples cited throughout are scale-leader/strategic comps (AuditBoard, SafeSend, Aareon, PowerSchool, etc.) that set unrealistic seller expectations; Banyan's actual entry band (~2-4x revenue / 8-15x EBITDA on proprietary sub-radar deals) is lower and mostly undisclosed.

  • Revenue-quality dilution must be screened, not assumed: hardware/telematics mix, services/implementation-heavy delivery, and license/support (vs. subscription) models can materially overstate true recurring-SaaS purity in several shortlisted names.
  • AI moat-erosion risk is real for buy-and-hold-FOREVER underwriting: VC AI-native entrants attack the exact sticky workflows that make these incumbents durable — favor slow-changing, regulation-anchored niches and stress-test defensibility over a multi-decade hold.

Market overview

Large, structurally fragmented, and growing above GDP. TAM depends heavily on scope: a narrow industrial-software cut is ~$20-60B, while the broad industrial software ecosystem is $100B+ (IoT Analytics: $146B in 2023 -> $355B by 2030, ~13.5% CAGR). The Banyan-relevant operating-workflow layer -- Manufacturing Operations Management (MES/MOM) -- was ~$21B in 2025 and is forecast to ~$110B by 2034 (~20.3% CAGR). Growth drivers: cloud migration of legacy on-prem ERP/MES, reshoring, labor scarcity, IT/OT convergence, regulatory traceability, and AI-in-workflow. Fragmentation is the defining feature at the workflow level: ERP, MES/MOM, QMS, CMMS/EAM, CPQ, PLM/BOM, SCADA and industrial IoT are bought by different personas, and discrete-vs-process manufacturing, sub-vertical specialization (metal fab, plastics, EMS, food, ETO/MTO job shops), and country-specific tax/compliance localization keep a very long tail of niche leaders alive. IoT Analytics notes MOM specifically has no vendor at double-digit share. This is exactly the "boring, embedded, mission-critical, recurring-revenue niche leader" profile Banyan hunts.

Consolidation state

Fragmented at the operating-workflow level, consolidating hard at the platform/capital level -- a two-speed market. Strategics (Siemens, Emerson/AspenTech, Schneider/AVEVA, Rockwell/Plex, PTC, Autodesk, Dassault, Hexagon, Synopsys/Ansys, Trimble, Honeywell) are assembling connected digital-thread platforms via multi-billion-dollar deals.
